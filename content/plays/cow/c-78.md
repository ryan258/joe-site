Mr. Big in a frightened person's hands: the kindness that is
also the case's best interview technique.

### When to use it

- Traumatized witnesses, frightened children, the rescued
  and the bereaved — people who cannot yet talk to
  adults with questions, and might talk *near* a toy.

### Mechanics

1. The gesture is genuine first (the sheet's warmth-
   through-practical-help is not a con — the con
   versions live in plays 26–40; this is the honest
   original): a scared child holding a warm old toy
   is *actually comforted* — a Fey spirit's presence
   is gentle by the sheet's whole backstory.
2. The investigative product arrives sideways: people
   soothed talk — and children talk *to toys*
   directly — while Cow's sonar (play 17) reads the
   fear's *shape* (fear of a person? a place? of
   being disbelieved?), which tells Joe what the
   eventual gentle questions should even be.
3. The discipline that keeps it honest: what's said
   to the toy in confidence is *weighed, not
   wielded* — fragments guide the investigation;
   they don't get quoted at the witness (J-79's
   rule) — and the comfort is never withdrawn as
   leverage. The toy stays as long as it's needed,
   case or no case.
4. The handoff to the interview proper (J-85): a
   witness who has spent an hour calm near Mr. Big
   meets Joe as "the kind man whose toy that is" —
   the single best interviewer's introduction the
   file owns.

### At the table

Mechanically: "She hasn't spoken since the fire —
so nobody asks her anything. Mr. Big just sits
with her. ...Cow reads the fear as
*person-shaped*, not fire-shaped: she saw
someone. And she's started whispering to the toy.
When she's ready, I'll be the man whose cow she
borrowed — and we'll talk about whatever she's
already told it."

Verbally: *"The girl won't speak to the watch,
the priest, or the kind lady from next door. But
in the burned house's doorway she sits with a
shabby toy cow against her cheek, whispering —
and the toy, who has been listening to
frightened children since before this town had
walls, holds every word like something
breakable."*
