The quarter's emotional weather, read from above: riots and
panics, forecast before they break.

### When to use it

- Cities under pressure: the party working a district
  where something is *building* — the read that gets
  the party (and the case's witnesses) off the street
  before the street becomes the event.

### Mechanics

1. The instrument at district scale: Cow's slow
   transits over the quarter (play 21's routes at
   survey pace) sampling the sonar (play 17) block
   by block — fear, anger, and their *flow* (fear
   radiating from a point is an incident; anger
   *converging* on one is a mob assembling — the
   map of feeling is a forecast in a way no street
   corner reads).
2. The visible correlates ride the same pass (plays
   1, 12): shops shuttering early, knots of men
   where knots don't form, the watch doubled or —
   worse — withdrawn (the authorities' own
   forecast, legible in their deployments).
3. The products, by urgency: *route intelligence*
   (tonight's movements re-planned around the
   pressure map); *witness protection* (the case's
   people moved before their street burns —
   J-137's escort with a deadline); and *the case
   itself* (who benefits from this quarter burning
   is sometimes the whole answer — manufactured
   mobs have paymasters, and converging anger
   often has a visible shepherd: follow him, play
   14).
4. The rail: forecast, never spark — the party's
   knowledge is for exits, evacuations, and
   evidence; the mob is a weather system, and
   nobody on this sheet steers weather at people
   (the Ideal; J-119's wildfire clause, urban
   edition).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Something's wrong in the tanners'
quarter — Cow flies the survey: sonar by block,
plus shutters, knots, and what the watch is
doing. ...Anger's *converging* on the
moneylender's row, the watch has pulled back to
the bridge, and one man is moving knot to knot
ahead of the flow. So: our witness moves to the
boathouse *now*, we're off these streets by
dusk — and I want that shepherd's face before we
go."

Verbally: *"To the men in it, the quarter is a
hot evening getting hotter. From the bell-tower,
the truth is meteorological: a storm of anger
with a human eye, walking knot to knot,
seeding — and one small watcher maps its track,
times its landfall, and carries the forecast
home in time to move everything that would have
burned."*
