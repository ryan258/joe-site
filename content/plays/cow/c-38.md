Mr. Big on the clerk's desk: embedded audit of ledgers,
ledger-keepers, and the money that moves after hours.

### When to use it

- Financial cases: embezzlement, the guild's two sets of
  books, the counting room where the fraud is performed
  daily at a desk.

### Mechanics

1. Insertion adapts the shelf craft (play 26) to the
   clerk's world: the desk toy is a *type* (clerks keep
   talismans against tedium) — arrival via the child's
   gift (play 28, the clerk's own child), the lost toy
   (play 30, rescued to the desk), or simple shelf
   tenure by the ledger cabinet.
2. What the perch reads (framed looks, play 19): *which*
   book comes out when — the second ledger's very
   existence is the case — plus after-hours visitors,
   the strongbox rhythm, and hands: who touches money,
   who touches records, who touches both (the fraud
   lives in the overlap; +10 converts, J-76).
3. Cow can't read columns (an owl's eyes, a spirit's
   attention — numbers travel poorly as images), so the
   brief targets *behavior around documents*, and Joe's
   own archive crawl (J-141) or authorized audit (J-142)
   lands on the pages Cow's watch identified.
4. Extraction on case-end via whatever inserted it —
   and the desk talisman's disappearance, noted by a
   guilty clerk, is itself a pressure play the interview
   (J-85) can spend.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow's third day on the counting desk:
the green ledger lives in the cabinet and comes out
only after the junior clerks leave — always with the
same two men present. I don't need Cow to read it; I
need exactly what I have: which book, which hours,
which hands. The magistrate's warrant does the
reading."

Verbally: *"The embezzlers work by lamplight, checking
the door twice, speaking in the counting-house murmur
that has hidden money for a thousand years. On the desk
between them, a child's toy watches the green ledger
open — again, and again, and again — with the patience
of compound interest."*
