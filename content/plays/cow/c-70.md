Guarding the mounts, the wagon, and the gear: the unglamorous
watch that keeps the party mobile.

### When to use it

- Every dismounted operation: the horses at the treeline,
  the wagon at the inn-yard, the packs cached at the cave
  mouth — the party's mobility and logistics, parked and
  vulnerable.

### Mechanics

1. The threat model, honestly ranked: theft (the
   commonest — horses are money on legs), scattering
   (the clever enemy's move: a party afoot is a party
   solved), and the mark (a watcher noting *whose*
   horses tells the enemy who's inside). Cow's watch
   answers all three at the price of none.
2. Station and brief: perched over the picket line,
   tiers loaded (play 67's ladder — fauna silent,
   approach pings Joe, hands-on-horses is *fire*);
   inside link range when the operation's geometry
   allows, advance-briefed with the *if* when not
   (play 25: *if hands touch the horses: fly to Joe
   now* — the ping mid-job is the party's cue that
   the exit just became contested).
3. The Fey-and-beast rapport (play 82's preview, DM's
   country): calm mounts under a familiar's presence
   is a common table ruling — horses that don't
   spook at the watcher above them, and a picket
   line that stays quiet under Cow's settled
   attention, which is itself operational value
   (screaming horses are an alarm pointed both ways).
4. The cache variant guards gear the same way: the
   party's packs, boat, or supply drop under the
   same tiers — and a *rifled* cache reported early
   (fragments name the rifler, play 25) converts a
   logistics loss into a counter-intelligence win
   (someone knew where to look: the leak is the
   case now, J-114's fourth clause).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Horses go on the picket at the
treeline, Cow goes on the branch above them —
standard tiers, and the hands-on-horses ping is
loaded. If anything touches that line while we're
inside, I know mid-corridor, and we adjust the
exit before it's a problem instead of after."

Verbally: *"The horse-thief works the picket line
with a professional's calm — soft hands, soft
words, a knife for the tether. The horses,
strangely, are calmer than he is: they've been
watched all evening by something old and gentle
in the branches, which is, at this exact moment,
telling a man three hundred feet underground
about the knife."*
