Cow over Joe himself: the nights the Keeper is the asset,
and the watcher watches its own.

### When to use it

- Inn rooms in hostile towns, solo travel, the nights
  after the party's made enemies with reach (J-100's
  stalkers, J-114's assassins) — Joe unconscious is
  Joe's whole kit unconscious; something must hold
  the watch.

### Mechanics

1. Station: the room's high vantage (wardrobe top,
   rafter, curtain rail) with sightline to door
   *and* window — inside link range by definition
   (the sheet's ping wakes the sleeping Keeper,
   play 67's confirmed ruling), tiers loaded with
   the inn's honest baseline (floorboards creak,
   drunks exist: tier calibration, play 67's
   night-three standard, runs on arrival night
   here).
2. The layered room (J-114's stack, bedroom
   edition): Alarm on the door's approach (J-31,
   ritual, free), the mundane tells (the chair
   under the latch, the flour line at the sill —
   play 50's powder in its domestic use), and Cow
   as the judgment tier over all of it — the
   assassin must beat three systems that share no
   failure mode, to reach a wizard whose *Shield*
   is pre-loaded (J-114's surprise-round clause).
3. The toy-mode variant for searched lodgings:
   Mr. Big on the nightstand *is* the watch —
   hosts who toss rooms find a sentimental toy
   beside a scholar's bed (play 26's provenance at
   its most natural), and the room stays guarded
   through the search itself.
4. The reciprocity is the play's quiet center (the
   sheet's bond, both directions): the Keeper
   guards Cow's existence; Cow guards the
   Keeper's sleep — and Joe, who plans everything,
   sleeps like a man who knows the arithmetic of
   his room is handled. Rest is a resource (every
   Long Rest play in the Joe file draws on it);
   the guard is how it banks.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Room's prepped: Alarm on the
hallway, chair under the latch, flour at the
sill, and Cow takes the wardrobe with sightline
to both entries. Tiers are calibrated for an
inn — drunks don't wake me, lockpicks do. I am
now going to sleep *well*, which tomorrow's
spell list will thank you all for."

Verbally: *"The assassin's craft is stairs that
don't creak and locks that don't complain, and
his craft is good. It is not good enough for
the flour, which remembers his boot; nor the
spell, which counts his approach; nor the two
golden eyes on the wardrobe, which have been
reading his intention through the door since
the stairwell — and whose Keeper is already,
calmly, awake."*
