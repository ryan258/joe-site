The discipline of keeping Cow grounded: knowing which
fights the bird sits out entirely.

### When to use it

- Before every deployment — the call is the *first*
  step of every combat play above, run as a
  checklist, not a feeling.

### Mechanics

1. The hard vetoes (any one grounds the bird, J-89
   parks it): **live or likely AoE** (enemy casters
   of unknown loadout count as likely until proven
   otherwise); **two-plus ranged attackers** with
   nothing better to shoot; **reach-weapon density**
   over the Help lanes; **weather** that degrades
   flight (the DM's storm outranks doctrine);
   **anything Fey-hostile or spirit-hungry** on the
   field (the sheet's stalkers, J-100 — Cow is
   never bait for its own hunters).
2. The soft factors, weighed not vetoed: the
   fight's stakes (a skirmish never justifies
   exposure a boss-fight might), the party's need
   (is the Help window worth more than the
   overwatch, play 55's trade), and the *ritual
   economics* (two incense portions in the pouch
   reads differently than none — J-33's ledger is
   a combat input).
3. Grounded ≠ useless: the parked bird re-enters as
   plays 61–62's tempo; the pack-carried bird runs
   plays 17–19 from Joe's shoulder-bag (sonar and
   census work fine from cover); toy-mode on Joe's
   belt is the fight's black-box recorder either
   way.
4. The call is announced (one word — "grounded" or
   "flying" — at initiative, J-96's script slot) so
   the party's plays that assume the bird (129,
   131, 133 in the Joe file) re-price instantly.
   Doctrine the table can hear is doctrine that
   works.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Initiative — and the call is
*grounded*: that's a robe I don't recognize plus
four archers with nothing to do. Cow rides my
satchel running sonar; you get pings, not
flybys, this fight. Senna, your Advantage comes
from my Web tonight, not the wings — adjust."

Verbally: *"The bird sits this one out — folded
in a satchel, reading the fight through feelings
while arrows own its sky. There is no glory in
the decision, which is precisely the point: the
Keeper's first oath was never *useful*. It was
*safe*."*
