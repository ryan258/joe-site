The high look at the sky, the road, and the day: route
intelligence that mundane parties buy with wet boots.

### When to use it

- Every travel morning (J-147's road doctrine owns a slot for
  it); before committing to passes, fords, and exposed country.

### Mechanics

1. Altitude is forecast: from 300 feet Cow reads the weather an
   hour of walking can't — the squall line behind the ridge,
   the river running high and brown upstream, the pass already
   white. Images carry it perfectly (*dark sky, fat river,
   white notch*).
2. The road audit rides the same flight: washouts, downed
   bridges, other travelers (their number, direction, and dust —
   company on the road is intelligence either way), and the
   ford's actual state versus the map's opinion of it.
3. Joe converts to decisions, not trivia: leave now or noon,
   this bank or that, camp short or push — the flight costs
   five minutes and re-prices the whole day.
4. In hostile country the weather eye doubles as the threat
   sweep (play 5's spokes, by daylight): the same pass that
   reads the sky reads the ambush country under it — J-147's
   whole product, airborne.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Morning flight says: squall over the west ridge
by midday, the ford's up to the markers already, and there's
wagon dust two miles behind us moving our direction. So we
cross now, camp early on the far bank, and I'd like to know
by supper who's been eating our dust since yesterday."

Verbally: *"The road argues for an easy morning. The sky,
consulted personally, disagrees — and the party that crosses
at nine watches the river close the argument at noon."*
