Downtime training: building the codebook, the behaviors, and
the trust that field plays spend.

### When to use it

- Downtime, camp evenings, road hours (J-147's rhythm) —
  the practice that turns plays 16, 52, 75, and 84 from
  clever ideas into reflexes.

### Mechanics

1. The codebook drills (play 16's maintenance):
   image-vocabulary rehearsed both directions —
   Joe sends, Cow confirms; Cow sends, Joe speaks it
   aloud — with the party present for the spoken
   half (they learn what "rain-smell, the four
   cups" sounds like *before* the night it matters).
   New signals adopted slowly: a vocabulary is
   strong at twelve words and mush at thirty.
2. The behavior drills (plays 52, 84): the flight
   patterns flown over camp until every party
   member reads circles-versus-figure-eights at a
   glance; the three-tap and the shoulder-landing
   rehearsed *on* each ally (the drill is also
   contact-comfort — the fighter who's been
   three-tapped in camp doesn't startle at it on
   a wall).
3. The scenario walks (the constitution rehearsed,
   play 94): the abort drill (Joe sends *fire*
   mid-exercise; Cow breaks off mid-task —
   instantly, every time, no completion instinct);
   the dead-man patterns (play 75) flown quarterly;
   the park-recall two-step (plays 61–62) timed
   until it's one smooth beat.
4. And the sessions are the bond's own time (the
   sheet's gentlest fact made schedule): drills
   end early when they end early; some evenings
   the practice is Mr. Big by the fire and
   nothing asked at all (play 92's asymmetry,
   deposited) — the trust the field spends is
   manufactured here, and only here.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Quiet week, so: drills. Monday
the codebook, both directions, party attending —
Senna, you'll hear the whole twelve-word
vocabulary spoken. Wednesday, flight patterns
over the meadow till everyone reads them at a
glance. Friday, the abort drill — and note
Cow broke off mid-circuit in under a second,
which is the only statistic in this campaign
I'm actually proud of. Weekend's off. Both of
us."

Verbally: *"Travelers passing the meadow see a
strange sight: a grey scholar and an owl,
running silent maneuvers by the hour — circles,
taps, sudden recalls — like a signal corps of
two. They do not see the evenings after, when
the drills are done and the ledger is shut,
and the whole curriculum is a toy cow
propped against a knee, being told, in no
language at all, that it did well."*
