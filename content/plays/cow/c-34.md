Mr. Big on the altar: insertion into shrines, cult rooms, and
the places where people say what they truly believe.

### When to use it

- Cult investigations and temple politics: the room where
  the faithful stop performing is the room the case needs —
  and offerings are the one category of object that
  *belongs* there uninvited.

### Mechanics

1. The mechanism: shrines accept objects — that is their
   function. A worn, loved toy left as an offering (grief
   reads as provenance: "someone's remembrance") joins the
   altar clutter with better tenure than furniture; clearing
   offerings is sacrilege in most traditions, and the DM's
   cult has traditions.
2. What altars hear (plays 17, 19, 24 from the one perch no
   one suspects): confessions — literal ones — plus the
   cult's operational talk after the laity leaves, the
   hierarchy visible in who stands where, and the back-room
   traffic behind the sanctuary.
3. The theological risk audit is mandatory: shrines are the
   one venue where *Detect Magic* sweeps and spirit-sensitive
   clergy are *likely* — J-88's sweep-veto applies at double
   weight, and Fey-hostile faiths are a hard no (Cow's
   safety outranks the case, the Keeper's constant).
4. Extraction: offerings can be "reclaimed by the grieving"
   with a donation — Joe's least favorite performance and
   his most convincing one.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The shrine's laity leaves at dusk and the
inner circle talks after — so the toy goes on the offering
shelf this afternoon, grief-provenance, two candles bought
to sell it. Risk check first: no house mage, and their
tradition venerates small spirits rather than hunting
them. Cow listens through tonight's 'private devotions.'"

Verbally: *"Among the shrine's offerings — the ribbons,
the widow's ring, the soldier's buckle — sits a child's
worn cow, the saddest and least questioned object on the
shelf, listening to the faithful with the oldest ears in
the room."*
