The multi-day static post: what it takes for Mr. Big to hold
one position for a week.

### When to use it

- Deep-cover placements (plays 26, 33, 34, 38) whose value
  compounds over days: patterns need repetitions, and
  repetitions need tenure.

### Mechanics

1. The spirit's endurance is the play's foundation: a Fey
   familiar doesn't hunger, tire, or bore the way flesh
   does (DM confirms the fiction once) — the *limits* are
   operational, not biological: link range, sweep risk,
   and the household's cleaning schedule.
2. The cleaning schedule is the real enemy: maids move
   toys, sell toys, give toys to nieces. Placement craft
   (play 26's dust-adjacency) plus a *watched first day*
   (does anyone touch the shelf?) prices the tenure before
   committing the week.
3. Joe's side of a long sit: periodic link-range passes
   (a lodger, a repeat customer, a scholar who walks that
   street — play 21's routes at daily scale) to collect
   live; otherwise the standing brief accumulates
   fragments against the extraction debrief (play 25) —
   with the codebook's *fire* as the always-loaded abort.
4. The week's product justifies the method: single looks
   see events; the long sit sees **routines** — and cases
   break on routines (the visitor who comes *every*
   third day is the case; the one who came once is an
   anecdote).

### At the table

Mechanically: "This needs a week, not a look. Day one I
watch the shelf get dusted — the maid works around the
clutter, good, tenure confirmed. I'm the lodger across
the lane; I pass link range twice a day for
collections. By day seven we won't know what happened
in that parlor — we'll know what *always* happens,
which is the thing warrants are made of."

Verbally: *"Seven days of the parlor's honest life:
the Tuesday visitor, the Thursday shouting, the drawer
that opens only when the mistress is at temple. The
little cow on the shelf gathers the household's
routine the way the shelf gathers dust — slowly,
totally, and without anyone once looking at it."*
