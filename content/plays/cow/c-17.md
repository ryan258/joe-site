Before sights and sounds, the link carries *feeling* — and a Fey
spirit feels a room the way owls hear mice.

### When to use it

- Ambiguous spaces: the too-quiet village, the friendly tavern,
  the meeting that might be a trap — anywhere the facts look fine
  and something itches.

### Mechanics

1. The standing query — "what does it *feel* like in there?" —
   is DM-mediated by design (the sheet makes Cow's impressions
   fragmentary and DM-controlled): the answer comes as
   emotion-tone, not data. *Fear* in a village that smiles;
   *hunger* behind a merchant's welcome; *nothing at all* where
   people should be feeling something — each is a finding.
2. Calibration honesty: sonar detects *presence and tenor*, not
   sources or reasons — it tells Joe where to point the real
   instruments (Investigation, the interview, play 20's ears),
   never what to conclude. The sheet's rule is the method's
   rule: impressions are evidence to weigh, not facts to
   assert (J-79).
3. Sweep protocol on entering any occupied space: one query at
   the threshold, one at the center, one at any person who
   pings — thirty seconds of feeling the room before trusting
   it.
4. The negative space finding again (J-140): a room whose
   emotional tone doesn't match its visual story — the merry
   feast that *feels* like a funeral — is the file's next case.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we sit down to their welcome-feast — Cow's
in my pack; threshold query. ...The room feels like held
breath. Everyone smiling up here, and the feeling says
'waiting.' We eat politely, we drink nothing, and I start
looking for what this village is waiting *for*."

Verbally: *"The feast is loud, warm, and generous — to the
eyes and the ears. To the small old spirit riding in the
scholar's satchel, the room tastes like the moment before a
trap closes, and it says so, in the language of held
breath."*
