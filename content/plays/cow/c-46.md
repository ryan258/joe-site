Mobile light aloft: the lantern-bearer who can't be
disarmed of it, for the party of humans in the dark.

### When to use it

- Joe's darkness problem (J-115) in motion: dungeon
  transits, night operations, the light that must move
  where hands are full or bodies can't go.

### Mechanics

1. The load: a lit candle-stub in a shielded carry (a
   tin thimble-lantern — one evening of tinkering, ounces
   total) rides the talon; a hooded lantern doesn't (a
   pound and awkward — the ruling from J-87 draws the
   line). The product: a light source at any point in a
   60-foot-per-round envelope.
2. The tactical geometry beats a carried torch three
   ways: the light stands *off* from the party
   (silhouetting enemies, not allies — J-115's
   asymmetry, airborne), moves *ahead* (the corridor's
   next turning lit before bodies reach it), and climbs
   (a raised light fills a chamber no hand-torch can).
3. The trade announced honestly: a flying light is a
   flying flag — this is the *loud* option, for when the
   party's location is already known or the dark is the
   greater enemy. The stealth alternative stays plays
   5/19: Cow sees, Joe rides, nobody shines.
4. Fire discipline rides along (J-119): a live flame at
   wing-height in web, hay, or gas country is the DM's
   favorite invitation — the carrier grounds it (a
   sill, a ledge) whenever the route's materials say
   so.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The hall's forty feet high and our
torches light ten of it — thimble-lantern flight:
Cow takes the candle up thirty and ahead twenty. The
frescoes, the gallery, and the two crossbowmen in it
are all suddenly in the light — and none of us are
holding it."

Verbally: *"A small flame ascends the dark like a
patient star, and the ambush in the gallery —
salaried, positioned, and invisible for six years —
finds itself lit from above by something no one can
shoot out of anyone's hand."*
