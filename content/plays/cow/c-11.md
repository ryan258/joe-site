Every exit of a building or compound, inventoried before the party
needs one — in either direction.

### When to use it

- Before entering anywhere the party might need to leave faster
  than it arrived; before watching anywhere a target might leave
  by other than the front.

### Mechanics

1. The census: doors (barred? hinged out or in?), windows large
   enough for a person, roof hatches, cellar chutes, the wall's
   climbable corner, the drain — flown as play 1's circuit with a
   burglar's eye instead of a soldier's.
2. Surveillance version: a watched front door with an uncensused
   back is theater — the census tells the party how many watchers
   a *real* watch on this target costs (usually: more than one
   party; hence Cow, play 4, who watches the back for free).
3. Escape version: the census pre-loads J-97 (retreat script) and
   J-61's *Dimension Door* targets — Joe visualizes exits he's
   effectively seen through the borrowed-eyes review (play 19).
4. The census ages fast (barrels stack, carts park, doors bar):
   re-fly it the day of use.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we take the meeting inside: Cow circuits
the guildhall. Every door, every man-sized window, the roof
trap, and whether the alley door bars from inside. If this goes
sour, I want to already know that the kitchen window is the
one that opens."

Verbally: *"The guildmaster knows his hall has three doors. The
owl on his weathervane knows it has nine, and has ranked
them."*
