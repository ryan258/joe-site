Hiding in the one place a toy is invisible: among toys.

### When to use it

- Toy shops, nurseries, orphanage shelves, festival stalls —
  and any investigation *of* those places (a smuggling
  ring's toyshop front; the nursery where the heir sleeps;
  the doll-maker whose customers frighten people).

### Mechanics

1. The concealment is categorical: on a shelf of forty
   toys, Mr. Big isn't disguised, it's *filed* — no search
   short of handling every doll finds the one that watches,
   and searches that thorough announce themselves streets
   away (which is also a finding: play 24's pings).
2. Nursery variant is a protection detail (play 28's ward-
   logic, stationary): the threatened child sleeps under
   Fey overwatch that reads as decor — the assassin-shaped
   problem meets play 23's held-breath detection with a
   100-foot silent alarm to wherever Joe sleeps.
3. Shop-front variant works the customers: who buys without
   browsing, who hands over notes with the coin, whose
   "purchase" weighs wrong — the front's real trade,
   logged from the display shelf (play 29's counter-
   sightline craft).
4. The extraction is retail: an ally buys the specific
   toy. Insert by donation, extract by purchase — commerce
   launders both directions.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The toyshop's a drop — someone's moving
notes through it. So: one more cow on a shelf of forty
animals, donated in a job lot. Cow logs which customers
don't browse. When we know the courier's face, Senna buys
a shabby toy cow for her niece, and the shop never had a
witness at all."

Verbally: *"The smuggler's shelf-boy dusts forty toys a
day and could not pick the new one from the stock if his
life required it — which, in the fullness of the court
record, it rather will."*
