Funerals, wakes, and memorials: the rooms where the case's
truth attends in mourning clothes.

### When to use it

- Deaths under investigation — the funeral is the one
  event every suspect attends, wearing their relation
  to the deceased where a Fey sense can read it.

### Mechanics

1. The premise (old investigator's truth): killers
   attend funerals — to perform grief, confirm the
   death, or watch the investigators — and
   *performed* grief differs from felt grief in
   exactly the dimension the sonar reads (play 17):
   the room's feeling versus the room's faces,
   mapped mourner by mourner per the DM.
2. Station respects the venue: the chapel eave, or
   toy-mode among the memorial offerings (play 34's
   provenance — grief placement is its most natural
   cover) — and the theological audit (play 34's
   sweep-veto) runs first at consecrated sites,
   always.
3. The reads that matter, briefed in advance:
   *absence of grief where it belongs* (the heir
   who feels nothing); *grief where none belongs*
   (the sobbing stranger — a secret relationship is
   a lead); *fear at the coffin* (of the dead? of
   discovery?); and *satisfaction anywhere* — the
   read that re-opens a verdict by itself.
4. The corroboration rule at full weight (J-79,
   heaviest here): grief is the least uniform of
   human feelings — the sonar *nominates* faces for
   the mundane file (who inherits, who argued, who
   left town: J-139's canvass runs on the
   nominees), and no one is accused of feeling
   wrong at a funeral. The read opens questions;
   the ledger closes them.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The whole household attends the
burial — so does Mr. Big, among the offerings.
Brief: absence, excess, fear, satisfaction.
...The widow's grief is a wound, genuine. The
brother's is painted over something that reads
like *relief*. Not an accusation — tomorrow's
archive pull: the estate's debts, in his name,
I'd wager. The feeling nominates; the paper
convicts."

Verbally: *"The eulogies are beautiful, the
black universal, and to every human eye the
household grieves as one. On the offering table,
beside the lilies, something small and old reads
the room's true weather — one wound, one
performance, one quiet unbearable relief — and
files each face under the feeling it actually
wore."*
