Cow's trust as evidence: the spirit's reactions to people,
kept as a ledger and weighed like any source.

### When to use it

- Every recurring NPC: the patron, the guide, the
  too-helpful stranger — Cow reacts to all of them, and
  the reactions, *logged over time*, form the party's
  least-foolable dossier.

### Mechanics

1. The instrument's nature (plays 17 and 79's
   reveal-ladder plus J-79's discipline, made a
   standing practice): a Fey spirit's read of
   people is old, pre-verbal, and aimed at
   *intention's texture* — not a lie detector (the
   sheet forbids that cleanly: fragments,
   DM-controlled), but a *consistency instrument*:
   the same person, read across meetings, in
   different masks.
2. The ledger is the method (J-83's ink, one page
   per person): each meeting's impression logged
   beside date and circumstances — and the
   *pattern* is the product: the patron who reads
   warm at contract time and cold at payment; the
   guide whose fear spikes at one bend of the
   road; the friend who read the same for a year
   and *changed last month* (the doppelganger
   case, J-105, opens with exactly this line).
3. The weighting rule, formalized: Cow's read is
   one witness — never the verdict (people are
   complicated; spirits misread; DMs curate) — it
   *nominates* (the cold patron gets the archive
   pull, J-141; the changed friend gets the
   private Message question, J-105), and the
   mundane evidence convicts or clears.
4. The reciprocal entry closes the practice:
   people's reactions *to Cow* — and to Mr. Big
   (who looks too long at the toy, J-100's
   ladder-trigger) — get the same page. The
   ledger reads both directions, and the
   stalker's first appearance in it is usually a
   footnote written weeks before anyone knew
   there was a case.

### At the table

Mechanically: "New patron, so the page opens
tonight: first meeting, Cow read him as
*careful, curious, no heat*. Fine — curiosity's
fair. If it's still 'no heat' at payment time,
better. And note: his clerk looked at Mr. Big
twice. Probably nothing. It goes on the page
anyway — the footnotes are where the cases
start."

Verbally: *"The party keeps many records: maps,
accounts, the case files. The strangest — and,
twice now, the most valuable — is a thin ledger
in Joe's smallest hand: one page per person,
one line per meeting, each line a feeling too
old to fool — in which, three weeks before the
betrayal, entry after entry, the warmth beside
one name quietly goes out."*
