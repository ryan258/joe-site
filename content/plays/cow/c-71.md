Unblinking custody: Cow over the captive between the
capture and the answers.

### When to use it

- Every prisoner the party holds — especially between
  the fight and the interview (J-85), the hours when
  captives escape, signal, self-harm, or get silenced.

### Mechanics

1. The watch's real product is *behavior before the
   interview*: what a captive does when they believe
   themselves unobserved — testing bonds (competent:
   note it), waiting calmly (professional: note it
   harder), mouthing toward the treeline (they have
   friends coming: the camp wants to know *now*) —
   is J-85's pre-loaded read, gathered while the
   party eats.
2. Tiers for custody (play 67's ladder, re-aimed):
   movement pings Joe, a *second party* approaching
   the prisoner is *fire* (the silencer is the
   classic — a captive worth holding is a captive
   someone wants un-held, and the approach is
   evidence arriving on legs, play 68's fork).
3. The emotional channel earns its keep here (play
   17): despair, patience, hope, and *timing* — the
   captive who calms at moonrise expects something
   at moonrise; the sonar reads the schedule their
   face hides, and the interview (J-85) opens with
   questions the captive believes unaskable.
4. The kindness rail holds (the sheet's, J-100's):
   the watch is custody, never torment — and Cow's
   presence, disclosed at Joe's choosing, is often
   the interview's best opener ("you've been
   guarded, not abandoned; nobody's coming to
   silence you on my watch" — protection as the
   first offer, J-143's product for captives).

### At the table

Mechanically: "The prisoner sleeps under the bird
tonight — tiers loaded, and the second-party ping
is set to fire: if anyone comes to shut him up,
that's the case walking into camp and I want
everyone up for it. And Cow says he goes calm
every time the moon clears the ridge. He's
expecting someone. The interview starts with
that."

Verbally: *"The captive waits out the night in
practiced calm, certain of the rescue that
always comes, watched — though he never learns
it — by two golden eyes that have already
counted his glances at the ridge, timed his
composure to the moonrise, and quietly
reported the whole appointment to the man
who'll greet his rescuers by name."*
