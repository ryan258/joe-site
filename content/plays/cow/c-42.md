Written words by air: the postal service for allies,
informants, and the split party.

### When to use it

- Messages past Message's 120 feet (J-11): the party split
  across a city, the informant who can't be visited (play
  143's garden), the warning that must arrive before Joe
  can.

### Mechanics

1. The medium: paper is featherweight — the note rides
   tied, tubed (a reed segment: rain-proofing is craft,
   not magic), or simply carried. Joe's hand writes it
   small; the calligrapher's kit makes tiny legible
   (J-83's tools in their gentlest use).
2. Security by physicality: an intercepted owl is nearly
   a non-event (birds are shot, not searched), but the
   *content* travels under the standard disciplines
   anyway — coded phrasing for sources (their name never
   on paper, play 143's protection product), and nothing
   written that hangs anyone if the reed falls in a
   river.
3. The recipient protocol scales by relationship: party
   members know the bird (fragments confirm delivery —
   Cow *saw* them take it); informants get the chalk-mark
   system's airborne upgrade (the note tells them where
   to chalk); strangers get nothing — Cow doesn't
   deliver to unvetted hands (the toy-provenance risk,
   J-100).
4. Return service is built in: the recipient's reply
   rides the same wings — a full correspondence loop, no
   streets walked, at 60 feet per round.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Tam's team is across the river with the
bridges watched — so the plan change goes by air:
reed-tube, small hand, 'the boathouse, not the mill,
midnight.' Cow knows Tam's face; I'll have
delivery-confirmed as a fragment inside the hour, and
Tam's reply by supper. The bridge watchers can watch
an empty bridge."

Verbally: *"The checkpoint reads every wagon, wallet,
and boot-sole that crosses the river. The conspiracy's
correspondence crosses forty feet above the
sergeant's hat, twice a day, on schedule."*
