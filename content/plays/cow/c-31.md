When the party's gear gets seized, Mr. Big goes along — the
sensor that gets arrested on purpose.

### When to use it

- Arrests (planned or survived), customs seizures,
  impoundments — any procedure that carries the party's
  belongings into the authorities' back rooms.

### Mechanics

1. The passive version is standing doctrine: Mr. Big *always*
   travels in Joe's effects looking sentimental and
   worthless (play 27's rider), so any seizure anywhere
   ships a sensor into the seizing institution's evidence
   room — the play costs nothing and is always armed.
2. Evidence rooms are intelligence bonanzas: what *else* is
   shelved there (the quarter's confiscations are the
   quarter's crime map), who visits (the corrupt clerk's
   withdrawals), and the paperwork said aloud over the
   ledger.
3. The active version stages the seizure: a party member
   gets deliberately, briefly arrested with the toy in
   their bundle (minor offense, fine paid tomorrow) —
   admission price for a night's surveillance inside the
   watch-house. Price the ethics and the risk like J-88:
   sparingly, and never where evidence gets *burned*.
4. Recovery rides the release procedure — effects returned,
   fragments debriefed (play 25) — or the claim visit (play
   27's paperwork) when procedure conveniently forgets.

### At the table

Mechanically: "They're seizing our packs at the gate —
irritating, and also: Mr. Big's in mine, per standing
doctrine. Our gear spends tonight in their evidence room,
which means *we* spend tonight in their evidence room.
Tomorrow we pay the fine, collect our effects, and learn
what the watch-house shelves next to its ledger."

Verbally: *"The customs sergeant inventories the wizard's
effects with contempt: books, ink, one shabby toy. He
shelves the toy beside eleven years of the port's
confiscated sins — and it reads the entire shelf before
midnight."*
