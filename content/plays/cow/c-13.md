Architecture from above: the drawing of a building's true shape
that its front door never admits to.

### When to use it

- Any structure the party will enter, fight in, or teleport
  around: the roof betrays the floor plan, and the floor plan is
  tactics.

### Mechanics

1. Roofs confess: wings and additions (different tile ages),
   interior courtyards invisible from the street, the count and
   position of chimneys (each one a room that matters), light
   wells, and the roof access the owner forgot counts as a door
   (play 11).
2. Joe converts overhead geometry to interior structure at the
   desk (+10; J-62's method: "floors ten feet apart, that wing's
   later, the vault sits under the one windowless quarter") —
   the *Dimension Door* and *Arcane Eye* briefs both start from
   this drawing.
3. Fights indoors run on it too: the party that knows where the
   building's spine and stairs are before entry fights J-96's
   script; everyone else fights the architecture.
4. One pass, drawn immediately (link-range sketch or post-flight
   fragments) — a roof plan from memory-of-a-memory is how
   walls end up ten feet from where the teleport thinks.

### At the table

Mechanically: "One high pass over the counting-house. Draw it
with me: main block, two wings — the north one's newer tile —
inner courtyard, five chimneys, and a windowless quarter on
the northeast that measures about twenty by twenty. That's
the strongroom, that's its ceiling height, and Dimension Door
now has an opinion about it."

Verbally: *"From the street, the counting-house is a modest
face and a strong door. From two hundred feet up, it is a
diagram of exactly what it's hiding, drawn in tile and
chimney-pots, legible to anyone who ever thinks to look
straight down."*
