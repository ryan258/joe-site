The default first move at any site: one slow lap of the outside before
anyone touches the inside.

### When to use it

- Every compound, camp, manor, ruin, or village the party intends to
  enter — before the approach is chosen, not after.

### Mechanics

1. Cow flies the boundary at 60 feet per round, high enough to be a
   bird and low enough to count: one circuit of a large manor takes
   under a minute of real time and costs nothing.
2. The deliverables, briefed in advance (images travel best as a
   list): entrances, sentries, light sources, animals (dogs undo more
   infiltrations than guards), and movement.
3. Inside 100 feet, Joe rides along live (play 19); beyond it, Cow
   flies the remembered brief and reports in fragments on return
   (play 25).
4. Perception +5 (Passive 15) and Stealth +5: the circuit notices and
   stays unobtrusive at a standard no human scout matches.
5. Altitude doctrine: above bowshot-casual (60+ feet), never a
   straight line to or from the party (the circuit ends somewhere
   innocent, then doubles back — birds that fly owner-patterns get
   remembered).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we pick an approach: Cow takes one lap at
seventy feet — entrances, sentries, lights, dogs, in that order.
I'll ride the link for the near half and take the report on the far
half. Two minutes."

Verbally: *"An owl crosses the vineyard at dusk, unremarkable as
weather — and by full dark, the manor's every door, dog, and
lantern is a small bright map behind a scholar's grey eyes."*
