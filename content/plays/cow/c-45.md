Bell ropes, hook latches, and tripwires: the mechanical
interventions a talon can make from the safe side.

### When to use it

- The mechanism is simple, reachable by air, and the wrong
  side of danger: alarm bells that need ringing (or not),
  latches that need lifting, the tripwire best tripped
  empty.

### Mechanics

1. The capability, honestly bounded: talons pull, lift,
   and drag at ounces of force — a hook latch, a light
   bolt, a bell rope, a hanging cord *yes*; a doorknob,
   a lever under spring load, anything needing grip-and-
   twist *no* (that's Mage Hand's 10 pounds, J-16 — the
   two tools split the mechanical world between them).
2. The alarm inversion: enemy bells rung at the *wrong*
   time (garrison scrambled to the empty east wall,
   J-23's misdirection without the slot) or friendly
   bells rung on schedule Joe can't keep — Cow becomes
   the signal infrastructure's ghost operator.
3. Trap service at range: the found tripwire (J-16's
   procedure) tripped by a diving pull with the party
   stacked clear — same discipline, blast-radius guess
   declared first, executed by the party member with
   wings and 1 HP *only* when the mechanism's throw is
   mapped (darts fly at man-height; the wire-puller
   dives *below* the plane — the geometry is briefed,
   not assumed).
4. The night-latch entry: the shutter hooked from
   inside, the storeroom's bar-hook — lifted at 3 a.m.
   by something that came down the chimney, and the
   party's entry is suddenly a door, not a breach.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The storeroom shutter hooks from
inside — Cow's down the cold chimney at three bells,
one talon-lift on the hook, and the window's just...
open. We walk in. And on the way out, Cow rings the
harbor bell — the garrison can spend our exit
investigating the empty docks."

Verbally: *"The night watch hears the harbor bell and
runs the wrong way, boots hammering toward nothing.
Behind them, a shutter swings silently open —
unhooked, as it were, by the building's own
conscience."*
