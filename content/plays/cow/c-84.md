Cow communicating to *others*: the expressive channel as
warning, guidance, and evidence for people who aren't Joe.

### When to use it

- The moments Cow must tell someone-not-Joe something —
  the ally beyond Message, the stranger who must be
  warned, the child who must follow *now* — using the
  sheet's other channel: expressive movement and
  behavior.

### Mechanics

1. The channel's honest bandwidth: no speech, no
   writing — but behavior carries *urgency,
   direction, affirmation, and alarm* natively: the
   insistent circling that means *follow*; the
   door-block that means *not that way*; the tug at
   the sleeve; the frozen stare at the danger's
   direction that every animal in every story has
   always meant one thing by.
2. The pre-briefed audience upgrade (party and
   trusted assets): two or three agreed behaviors
   (*lands on your left shoulder* = come to Joe;
   *three taps* = danger, hide; *the bow* = all
   clear) — a codebook-lite (play 16) for people
   the link can't reach, drilled once, worth a
   postal service in moments that can't wait for
   one (play 42).
3. The stranger case runs on instinct's universals:
   people *follow* an insistent animal — especially
   children, especially in danger (the folklore is
   load-bearing; the DM's world has the same
   stories ours does) — the play is Cow leading the
   lost child out of the burning quarter when no
   human rescuer can reach them.
4. The testimony variant, used sparingly: Cow's
   behavior *toward a person* — the recoil, the
   refusal, the placed distrust — read by Joe as
   one more nominated impression (J-79's weight,
   play 86's ledger), and occasionally *shown*
   deliberately: the toy a dozen witnesses watch
   refuse a man's hand plants a true question in a
   room. Honest theater, on the sheet's own
   channel.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Senna's past every channel I own
and the ambush is *ahead* of her — Cow flies it:
three taps on the shoulder, our drilled 'danger,
hide.' She knows the code. ...And she's gone to
ground. The bird just outran the trap by ninety
seconds of vocabulary we agreed on at
breakfast."

Verbally: *"The child stands lost where the
smoke forks the lane, and something small and
impossible lands before her — circling,
insistent, tugging her sleeve toward the river
with the whole authority of every story her
grandmother ever told her. She follows. Of
course she follows. The stories were about
this."*
