The routed enemy, followed home: every fight's survivors are
free intelligence, if someone tracks the run.

### When to use it

- Enemies break and run — the moment most parties cheer
  and lose the campaign's best lead. The runner knows
  where the base, the boss, and the next ambush live.

### Mechanics

1. The launch is automatic doctrine: the round the rout
   starts, Cow lifts and takes the runner (play 14's
   trail eye at combat tempo) — no order needed; the
   standing brief covers it (*if they run: follow,
   perch, count, home*, play 25's grammar).
2. The party finishes the fight unhurried: the mark's
   trail doesn't cool at wing-speed the way it cools
   at boot-speed — pursuit stops being a now-or-never
   sprint (J-58's tactical version) and becomes an
   afternoon's walk to a known address.
3. The destination gets the full treatment on arrival:
   perch and census (plays 3–4) *before* Cow returns —
   the report isn't "they went northeast," it's "a
   mill, nine men, one man they reported to, two dogs."
4. The counter-intelligence read rides free: a runner
   who *doesn't* run home — who circles, waits,
   watches the party — is a scout, not a coward, and
   knowing the difference (Cow's follow reveals it)
   re-prices the whole encounter as reconnaissance
   the enemy just spent lives on.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Two of them are rabbiting — let them.
Cow's already up and on the left one. We loot,
bandage, and walk it in an hour. ...Report: a mill,
two miles, nine more men, and our runner reported
to a grey-robe on arrival. So the ambush had a
mother. Tomorrow, so do we."

Verbally: *"The survivor runs three miles of clever
country, certain he's carried the warning safely
home — never once looking up at the small shadow
pacing him, which has already counted the mill's
men, marked its dogs, and memorized the face he
reports to."*
