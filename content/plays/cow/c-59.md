Watching for the second wave: the standing combat station
that assumes every fight has more fight behind it.

### When to use it

- Structured enemies (garrisons, cults, war-bands): the
  first contact is rarely the whole roster, and the
  reserve's arrival timing decides more fights than any
  single spell.

### Mechanics

1. Station and brief: Cow holds high over the *likely
   reinforcement axis* — the barracks door, the road,
   the tunnel mouth (the recon plays, 1 and 13, said
   where those are) — with the one-job brief:
   *rain-smell plus count the moment anything moves
   this way*.
2. The warning's value is measured in rounds: two
   rounds of notice converts J-97's retreat from rout
   to procedure, or converts the reserve's arrival
   *into the ambush* — Joe's Web pre-laid across
   their door (J-2), the Ice Storm's ground waiting
   on their muster (J-59), the fight's second half
   fought on the party's blueprint.
3. The negative report is load-bearing: *still water*
   from the reserve axis is the license to spend
   everything on the fight in front — no slots held
   against ghosts (J-73's budget hates insurance
   against nothing).
4. Rotation with the caller (play 55): one bird, one
   sky — the station covers *the* likely axis, not
   all of them; Joe picks it like he picks Fireball
   centers (the read is the skill), and the party's
   own ears own the rest.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we hit the gatehouse: Cow takes
the barracks axis — that door, nothing else, count
and direction the second it opens. If we get the
ping, the Web goes across their doorway before
they're through it, and the reserve reinforces a
corridor full of silk. If we get still-water, I'm
spending everything forward. Either way, we knew."

Verbally: *"The garrison's doctrine is sound: hold
the gate, bleed them, commit the reserve at the
turning point. The doctrine does not survive the
reserve door opening into twenty feet of patient
silk — laid there ninety seconds earlier, on the
word of the sky."*
