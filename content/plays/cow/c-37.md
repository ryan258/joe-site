Joe imprisoned, with his toy: the incarceration kit that
turns a cell into a listening post.

### When to use it

- Arrests and captures — planned (play 31's active
  version), risked, or survived. Prisoners keep less than
  their clothes; they surprisingly often keep sentiment.

### Mechanics

1. The intake bet: jailers confiscate weapons, coin, and
   tools — a shabby toy clutched by a cooperative,
   harmless-reading scholar frequently rides through (and
   if it doesn't, play 27's confiscation gambit begins
   instead; the fork wins either way).
2. Inside, the kit unfolds: Cow slips the bars at night
   (Tiny, silent — a cell holds a man, not a bird) and
   runs the jail: the guard rotation (play 9), the
   corridor map (play 13's logic indoors), the warden's
   office ears (play 7), the other prisoners' cells —
   who's held here, which is its own case file.
3. The link makes Joe the best-informed man in the
   building: escape planning with full intelligence
   (J-97's script wants the map Cow drew), or —
   often better — *staying* informed until the party's
   lawful lever (J-142) or unlawful one (J-84's papers)
   opens the door.
4. The outbound courier lives in the kit: Cow flies the
   party a message fragment (play 25 to wherever they
   are), and the isolation that makes prisons work is
   simply off.

### At the table

Mechanically: "They took my belt and my books and left
the toy — jailers always do. So: tonight Cow slips the
bars and clocks the rotation; by dawn I'll have the
corridor map and the warden's evening schedule; and Tam
gets a fragment by owl — 'alive, north cell, third
night.' I'm not imprisoned. I'm embedded."

Verbally: *"The cell door slams on a docile scholar
holding a child's toy — the jail's most harmless intake
in a year. By the third night, the prison has no
secrets, the warden has no privacy, and the harmless
man in the north cell has a map, a schedule, and a
postal service."*
