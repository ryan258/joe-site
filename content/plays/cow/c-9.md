Sentry rotations measured to the minute: the timing intelligence
that turns walls into doors.

### When to use it

- Before any timed infiltration (J-82's crossing windows, J-126's
  volley rhythms): the patrol's period is the job's metronome.

### Mechanics

1. Cow perches through **two full cycles** minimum (one cycle
   tells you a pattern exists; two tell you its period and its
   slop) — logging in image-beats: *guard at the corner; count;
   guard at the corner again*.
2. Joe converts beats to minutes at the desk and extracts the
   product: the gap (where and how long), the overlap (never
   there), the lazy leg (every patrol has one), and the shift
   change (the golden minute when everyone's watching each other
   clock out).
3. Weather and hour re-cut the clock — rain shortens legs,
   midnight lengthens them; the clock is re-run the night of the
   job, not trusted from last week (stale census, play 3's rule).
4. The deliverable is one sentence to the party: "the northwest
   corner is empty for ninety seconds after each bell" — the
   whole surveillance apparatus compressing into a crossing
   window (J-82 executes it).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow's watched two full rotations: four men, a
twelve-minute period, and the granary leg takes them blind of
the north wall for about ninety seconds — longer since the
rain started. Shift change at the bell doubles it. That's our
window, and we re-verify it tomorrow night before we use it."

Verbally: *"The watch walks its round as it has for eleven
years, and its round has never once been wrong. It has also
never once been timed — until the night something patient sat
above the granary, counting."*
