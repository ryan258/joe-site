The Help flyby, aimed at the enemy mage: Advantage delivered
against the target whose defenses are spells, not steel.

### When to use it

- An enemy caster behind the lines: often high-value,
  low-AC, and guarded by distance rather than armor — the
  profile Cow reaches and martials don't.

### Mechanics

1. The flight: enemy backlines are usually *air-open* —
   Cow's approach at altitude crosses the melee nobody
   can leave, and the Help lands on a robe with AC 12
   whose bodyguards are all facing the other way.
2. The beneficiary math: Advantage against a caster
   feeds the party's *ranged* attackers (the archer,
   Joe's own attack cantrips — Joe can claim his own
   Cow-window: Advantage on Fire Bolt is +4-ish to hit
   *and* crit-fishing for 4d10) — announce the window
   toward the shooters (play 53's rule).
3. The secondary product is disruption without damage:
   a caster with wings at their face plays worse —
   many DMs price the distraction into the NPC's
   choices (repositioning, defensive casting), which
   is tempo the party never pays for. Not a rules
   guarantee; a table reality.
4. The veto list is longer here (the 1 HP audit,
   sharpened): casters *are* AoE, and a robe that's
   noticed the bird owns *Thunderwave*-shaped answers —
   one pass per fight against unknown casters, and
   the *fire*-abort pre-loaded (play 25).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Their warlock's untouchable behind the
shield wall — for people who walk. Cow's turn: over
the melee, Help at his eyes, exit high. Window's

Verbally: *"The warlock's defenses are geometry:
forty feet, a wall of shields, the whole grammar of
the battlefield between him and harm. The grammar
does not parse owls — and the fire that follows the
wings arrives with its whole argument doubled."*
