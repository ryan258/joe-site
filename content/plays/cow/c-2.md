Mapping ground the party can't walk: a methodical aerial survey,
flown in lanes.

### When to use it

- Large areas where the objective's *location* is the question: a
  ruin field, a forest camp somewhere in a valley, the block where
  the safehouse hides.

### Mechanics

1. Lanes, not wandering: Cow flies parallel passes 100–150 feet
   apart (Darkvision 120 and owl eyes overlap the strips), turning
   at agreed landmarks — a search pattern briefed as images: *the
   river, the ridge, back, again*.
2. Joe plots the returns on paper in real time where the link holds,
   or after each leg where it doesn't — the journal page becomes the
   map (J-140's ink discipline, applied to geography).
3. The sweep finds *anomalies*, not answers: the too-regular
   clearing, the smoke without a village, the horses where no road
   goes. Anomalies get a dedicated circuit (play 1) next.
4. Night sweeps trade human-spotting risk for darkvision (play 5);
   dawn and dusk sweeps catch camps at their loudest — cookfires
   and movement (play 12).

### At the table

Mechanically: "The bandit camp is somewhere in six square miles of
valley. Cow flies lanes off the river, a ridge-to-ridge pass every
hundred fifty feet. I want smoke, horse-lines, and geometry nature
doesn't do. We'll have a dot on this map by midday."

Verbally: *"The valley keeps its secret from the road, from the
ridge, from every mounted patrol the baron ever paid for — and
gives it up in an afternoon to something small flying very
straight lines with great patience."*
