Cow as the fight's air-traffic control: round-by-round
intelligence while the party's eyes are full of enemies.

### When to use it

- Fights with off-screen movement: reinforcements,
  flankers, the enemy that broke contact — every combat
  bigger than the room it started in.

### Mechanics

1. Station: altitude over the fight's edge (not its
   center — AoE, play 66), covering the approaches the
   party's engaged eyes can't (play 18's wedge at
   battle scale). No action spent: *watching* is free;
   the link's pings are thought-speed (play 16's
   codebook doing combat duty: *rain-smell + landmark*
   = movers, direction).
2. Joe's dispatch role: pings convert to party warnings
   as free-action speech on his turn ("four more,
   ravine side, two rounds out") — the fight's J-96
   script updates live, and nobody gets flanked in a
   fight the sky is calling.
3. The Bonus-Action look (play 19) spends Joe's Bonus Action only
   at decision points — *is that a second wave or a
   rout* — priced against his cantrip like any action
   (J-133's economy: information that changes the
   party's round beats 2d10 into it).
4. Compatible with Help passes (play 53) on the same
   deployment: the caller's station *is* the flyby's
   home altitude — watch by default, dive when the
   window's worth more than the coverage gap.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow holds sixty feet over the barn —
watching the road and the ravine while we're all
staring at axes. ...Ping: rain-smell, ravine. 'Four
more, ravine side, two rounds!' — Tam, your flank.
See, that's why the bird doesn't fight: the bird
*dispatches*."

Verbally: *"The reinforcements come clever and
quiet, up the ravine the defenders can't watch —
and are met, on arrival, by a line that turned to
face them twelve seconds ago, on the word of the
one combatant in the valley with nothing in its
eyes but everything."*
