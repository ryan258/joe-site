Operations past 100 feet: the advance-brief grammar that lets
Cow work alone, and the debrief craft that recovers what it saw.

### When to use it

- Whenever the job outranges the link: deep scouts (plays 2,
  14), courier runs (J-87), independent watches — most of
  Cow's range, most of the time.

### Mechanics

1. Outside 100 feet there is no live channel — Cow flies on
   **instructions given in advance**, and the sheet's grammar
   governs: brief in images and intentions, simple and few.
   Three clauses is a long order (*the red-curtain window;
   watch; return at dawn*). Contingencies compress to one
   *if* (*if fire: come home now*).
2. The debrief recovers fragments, not footage: Cow returns
   with remembered sensations the DM curates — Joe's craft is
   the *interview* (framed questions, play 19's discipline,
   applied retrospectively: "show me hands" / "show me the
   door") and the immediate inking (fragments decay in Joe's
   memory, not the journal's).
3. Mission design respects the medium: send Cow for things
   images hold well (counts, colors, faces, geometry, the
   feel of a place) — never for words beyond a name or two,
   never for judgment calls past the one *if*.
4. The range-planning habit: know where the tether's edge
   falls on the map *before* launch — many "beyond tether"
   jobs become tether jobs (play 21) if Joe walks two
   hundred yards, and live coverage always beats recall.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The tower's a half-mile out — beyond the link,
so we brief simple: the tower; the door; count boots;
home by moonrise; if fire, home now. When Cow's back I'll
run the debrief — hands, doors, feelings, in that order —
and ink it before we discuss. And note the tether edge on
the map: from the mill, this becomes a live job."

Verbally: *"The little spirit flies out past the reach of
any voice, carrying its orders the only way it can — as
three pictures and a feeling — and comes home at moonrise
carrying the tower the same way: in fragments, like pages
saved from a fire, for the patient man who knows how to
rebind them."*
