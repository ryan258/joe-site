Mid-combat pocket-dimension timing: the dismissal as a
combat maneuver, not just a pre-fight habit.

### When to use it

- The fight's character changes mid-stream: the enemy
  caster reveals AoE, the second wave brings archers,
  the melee sprawls toward Cow's station — J-89 parks
  before fights; this play parks *during* them.

### Mechanics

1. The mechanic's shape: Joe's **Magic action**
   dismisses Cow to the pocket dimension from any
   distance... within the bond's terms (the sheet and
   J-89 govern; the recall is within 30 feet of Joe).
   The cost is Joe's action — a full turn's cantrip
   traded for the bird's existence. The trade's rule:
   **the action is cheaper than the ritual** (one
   hour and 10 GP, J-33) every single time.
2. The trigger list, pre-committed (decision fatigue
   kills birds): enemy caster *seen casting anything
   area-shaped*; two or more ranged attackers
   acquiring the sky; the fight's zone sprawling
   under Cow's station; any *fire*-ping of Cow's own.
   Any one trips the park — no round of "maybe it's
   fine."
3. The re-deploy is a play of its own (J-89's
   30-foot recall geometry): parked isn't gone —
   the fight's back half often wants Cow *elsewhere*
   (the reserve axis, play 59; the rout-follow, play
   58), and the park-recall two-step is also a
   *reposition* that crosses the battlefield
   untouchably.
4. The accounting habit: a fight Cow spends parked
   is a fight the party fought without plays 53–60 —
   fine, and priced. The point of doctrine is that
   the *bird* is never the thing the fight costs.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Their backup's a robe and the robe
just fanned her fingers — trigger one. My action:
Cow's parked, mid-flight, before whatever that is
finishes. I lose my cantrip this round and keep
the bird. When the robe's dealt with, Cow recalls
behind the wall and takes the rout-watch."

Verbally: *"The sorceress shapes her storm with a
craftswoman's patience, angling it to catch the
irritating bird — which stops existing halfway
through her gesture, folded gently out of the
world by a man who has done this arithmetic
before, and has never once gotten it wrong."*
