Mr. Big, sold into the fence's shop — the sensor that
inventories the stolen-goods trade from the inside.

### When to use it

- Fencing investigations: the shop that moves the quarter's
  stolen goods, the pawnbroker whose back room is the case.

### Mechanics

1. Insertion: a party ally (never Joe — the fence will
   remember faces) pawns the toy among a plausible bundle of
   shabby goods. Toys are pawnshop wallpaper: shelved,
   ignored, and positioned — by trade logic — with sightline
   to the counter where every transaction happens.
2. The watch is a transaction ledger (plays 19, 24): faces,
   goods, the back-room traffic, and *which customers get
   walked past the counter* — the fence's real clients never
   see the shop's front ledger.
3. Duration is priced by the pawn ticket: the loan period is
   the surveillance window, and redemption (the ally returns,
   pays, collects) is the built-in, unremarkable extraction —
   with the entire week's traffic in fragmentary memory.
4. The risk audit before insertion: fences *appraise* — a
   worn toy invites no appraisal magic, but a shop with a
   house mage gets the J-88 sweep-rule veto. And the abort
   image rides loaded, as always.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Senna pawns Mr. Big in a basket of junk —
two silver, one week's ticket. The toy shelves out facing
the counter. For seven days it logs every face, every
bundle, and everyone who gets waved through the curtain.
Then she redeems the ticket, and we read the shop's real
books."

Verbally: *"The fence prides himself on knowing the value
of everything that crosses his counter. He shelves the one
priceless thing he'll ever handle at two silver, facing
out, with a clear view of all his sins."*
