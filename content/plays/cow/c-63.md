Drawing the enemy's readied actions: the deliberate
exposure — priced, rare, and occasionally correct.

### When to use it

- The enemy holds readied ranged attacks that will
  otherwise land on the party's *breach* — the held
  crossbow covering the door, the overwatch that
  punishes the first body through. Someone triggers
  it; the question is who can afford to.

### Mechanics

1. The brutal arithmetic, stated first: a readied
   bolt that hits Cow deals its damage to **1 HP** —
   the bird dies, full stop, and costs an hour and
   10 GP (J-33). The play is *never* casual: it runs
   only when the same bolt landing on a person costs
   more (the wounded ally breaching, the rogue at 4
   HP) *and* no bloodless trigger exists (Minor
   Illusion's fake entry, J-13, is always auditioned
   first — a sound is cheaper than a spirit's form).
2. The execution stacks every survival edge: Flyby
   entry at speed across the covered zone's *edge*
   (not its heart), erratic line (readied attacks
   against a Tiny, fast, jinking target are the
   worst shot the enemy will take all day — AC 11
   plus the DM's honest circumstance is a real miss
   chance), exit immediate.
3. The payoff window is the party's to spend: the
   volley triggered, the overwatch empty, the breach
   goes *now* — the whole play is converting the
   enemy's best-prepared round into a spent one, on
   the party's clock (J-131's window logic,
   purchased with feathers).
4. And the aftermath is honored either way: a
   surviving Cow gets the fight off (parked, play
   61 — one bait per engagement, ever); a lost form
   gets the ritual that night, the incense line in
   the ledger, and Joe's quiet, genuine apology —
   the sheet's bond is not a rules fiction, and the
   table should see the Keeper pay the cost he
   authorized.

Verbally: *"Two bolts, held for a killing minute
against the first thing through the door, spend
themselves instead on six inches of impossible
jinking feathers — and the door, in the empty
half-second after, fills with fighters who were
never going to be shot at all. High above, the
little spirit shakes out its wings once,
professionally, and declines an encore."*
