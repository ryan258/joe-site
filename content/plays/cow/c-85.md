Cow as the party's heart: the mascot function, taken
seriously as table craft.

### When to use it

- The campaign's low nights — after the lost fight, the
  dead NPC, the burned village — and the long gray
  middles where parties fray. Not a con, not a spell:
  the real function of a beloved small creature among
  tired people.

### Mechanics

1. The honest mechanism: morale is real at every
   table (and many DMs price it — Heroic
   Inspiration awards follow good scenes, J-71's
   currency), and a shabby toy cow that is also an
   ancient gentle spirit is a *morale engine*: Mr.
   Big propped solemnly against the stew-pot; the
   owl asleep on the big fighter's helmet; the
   head-tilt at exactly the pompous moment.
2. The deliberate deployments: the act (play 79)
   turned *inward* on the party's worst evenings;
   the watch handed visibly to the bird ("sleep —
   Cow has it," play 67) as the *permission to
   rest* tired people can't give themselves; the
   small rituals (Mr. Big travels in
   whoever's-grieving's pack this week — the toy's
   own history is being given to a child who
   needed it, and the sheet remembers).
3. The character logic runs deep: Joe shows warmth
   through practical help and his gentlest humor
   around Cow (the sheet's exact words) — the
   party reading Joe *through* Cow is how the
   quiet man's affection becomes visible; the
   mascot is also the Keeper's translator.
4. The rail that keeps it true: never performed
   cynically at the party (they'd feel it, and
   the DM should let them) — the play is *noticing
   when it's needed*, which is the investigator's
   instrument (play 17) pointed, for once, at
   home.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Rough day, and tomorrow's worse —
so tonight: Cow has the watch, visibly, all
tiers, and everyone actually sleeps. Mr. Big
rides in Brenna's pack this week — no reason,
except every reason. And if the solemn stew-pot
vigil earns anyone Heroic Inspiration at this
table, I'd argue it's earned honestly."

Verbally: *"The party sits silent around the
fire with the day's losses, and the toy cow
stands its solemn crooked guard against the
stew-pot — patient, absurd, undefeated — until
the fighter snorts, and the snort becomes a
laugh, and the laugh goes around the fire like
something being handed back to each of them,
one at a time."*
