The foundation courier flight: small metal, moved through air
that walls don't govern. (J-87 wrote the doctrine; this play
is the checklist.)

### When to use it

- Keys, rings, coins, lockpicks — ounces of metal that
  change everything on the far side of a wall, a moat, a
  cell door.

### Mechanics

1. The checklist, run every flight: **load** (talon-carry,
   ounces — the DM's ruling from J-87 governs; a key ring
   yes, a dagger no); **route** (night and altitude
   preferred, play 8's dark-sky logic; never the straight
   line, play 1's doubling habit); **recipient** (expecting
   an owl, or the delivery is an incident — pre-arranged
   signal, or Message inside 120 feet, J-11); **exposure
   clock** (total time in hostile air, minimized by route
   planning before launch).
2. The drop technique: through bars, onto sills, into
   hands — Flyby means the delivery pass never stops
   moving; a hovering delivery is a shot opportunity
   (no-Hover is also just good doctrine).
3. The abort lives in the brief (play 25's *if*): hawks,
   archers, or lights tracking the approach — drop
   nothing, come home, re-plan. A key delivered to a
   compromised recipient arms the enemy.
4. Two-way traffic doubles the value: the same flight that
   delivers the picks extracts the prisoner's note, the
   wax impression, the stolen seal (play 44).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cell key to the north window, forty feet
up: night flight, over the roofline not the yard, one
pass — drop through the bars moving, no perch. She knows
to expect wings at moonrise. Total exposure about ninety
seconds, and if there's a light on that wall, Cow comes
home with the key and we go to plan two."

Verbally: *"The prison's walls are proof against
ladders, ropes, bribes, and tunnels — a fortress of
categories. The key crosses them all in a category the
architects never budgeted for: ninety seconds of
patient, silent wings."*
