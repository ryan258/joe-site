Numbers decide plans. Cow counts what the party is actually facing
before the party decides anything.

### When to use it

- Any known enemy position, before every assault, parley, or
  avoid-entirely decision — the census is the cheapest intelligence
  in the game.

### Mechanics

1. The count is a *sustained* task, not a glance: Cow perches over
   the camp (play 4) through a meal or a shift change — the moments
   a force shows its true number — and counts in the terms images
   carry: *hands around fires, bedrolls, horses, boots outside
   tents*.
2. Bedrolls and horses don't lie; head counts do (sentries double-
   counted, sleepers missed). Brief Cow to count *things*, and Joe
   converts to people at the desk (+10 Investigation on the
   arithmetic of a camp, J-76).
3. The census includes quality: armor glint versus rag, one big
   tent versus none, a caster's paraphernalia — images Cow can
   carry (*shine, size, the smell of strange smoke*).
4. Recount before executing: forces change, and yesterday's twelve
   is tonight's forty. The plan drawn from a stale census is J-97
   waiting to happen.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we vote on fighting them: Cow sits their
supper. I want bedrolls, horses, fires, and boots — things, not
faces. ...Fourteen bedrolls, nineteen horses. Someone's expecting
five more, or five are out right now watching something. Maybe
us."

Verbally: *"The bandits count themselves rich and many. Above
them, through a lattice of pine, something counts them
precisely."*
