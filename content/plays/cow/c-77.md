Cow reading the crowd for the watcher: counter-surveillance
at the public event.

### When to use it

- Markets, festivals, hangings, sermons, auctions — the
  public moments when the party is visible and someone
  in the crowd may be working: the pickpocket, the
  spotter, the face from the case (J-138's craft,
  pointed at an audience instead of a route).

### Mechanics

1. The vantage does the work: from the eave-line, a
   crowd is a *pattern* — flows, eddies, and the
   figures who violate them (the man who doesn't
   watch the hanging; the woman circling against
   the drift; the stillness in the moving mass —
   watchers watch, and watching has a posture
   legible from above).
2. The brief is shape-first (play 25's grammar for
   crowds): *who watches us; who doesn't move; who
   follows when we do* — three questions Cow's
   +5 Perception answers from one perch,
   fed live where the link reaches (the party
   strolling inside Cow's 100-foot coverage: play
   21's bubble, stationary).
3. The emotional layer runs underneath (play 17 at
   crowd scale): fear moving through a market
   ahead of something; the *intent* cluster near
   the stage; the wrongness with a location — the
   sonar doesn't name the pickpocket, it names
   the *block* to watch, and the eyes do the rest.
4. The product forks by find: the spotter gets the
   counter-tail fork (play 69 — starve, follow, or
   feed); the pickpocket gets Joe's dry warning to
   the party (and the *bait purse*, if the team's
   feeling like fishing — the lifted decoy
   followed home is a guild's address, play 30's
   travel-inward logic); the face from the case
   gets everything — perch, follow, and the file
   (plays 4, 14, and J-140's ink).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Festival day, so we're visible —
Cow takes the clock-tower eave over the square.
Standing scan: who watches us, who isn't
watching the puppets, who moves when we move.
...Ping: one, by the fountain, still as
furniture, and the crowd's fear-eddy bends
around the ale tent — so that's a watcher *and*
a separate problem. Watcher first. Nobody look
at the fountain."

Verbally: *"Ten thousand people watch the
festival. Three people watch the party. One
small shape above the clock face watches the
three — sorting the whole bright roaring
square, face by face, into the innocent, the
working, and the one man by the fountain who
has made the oldest mistake in his
profession: holding still in a world that
moves."*
