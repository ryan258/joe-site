Rivers, docks, sewer mouths, and cisterns: the wet approaches
nobody watches, surveyed from the air.

### When to use it

- Cities and fortifications: the water is always a door — the
  smuggler's, the escape's, or the enemy's — and it's the
  least-watched face of anything.

### Mechanics

1. Cow flies the waterline at dusk: outfall positions, dock
   activity after hours, boats that don't fish, the culvert with
   the worn path to it, water-gates and their chains (up or
   down — an image Cow carries perfectly).
2. Depth and current read from above by proxy: wading birds,
   moored boats' swing, the debris line — Joe converts (+10)
   into "crossable / boatable / neither."
3. Watch the *watchers* of the water: a guarded dock is
   somebody's asset; an unguarded one is everybody's door.
   Both are findings.
4. The survey feeds three files at once: entry options (J-148's
   exits inventory, inverted), the smuggling case (boats that
   don't fish are boats that carry), and the retreat plan
   (J-97's rally points love a boat).

### At the table

Mechanically: "While the town eats dinner: Cow flies the
riverfront, bridge to bridge. Culverts, water-gates, boats
moored where boats don't work, and whichever dock has a guard
who isn't advertised. The wall has a front door and a price;
the river usually has neither."

Verbally: *"The city watches its gates with forty men and its
river with a chain it hasn't checked since the flood. The
chain, an owl notes in passing, is down."*
