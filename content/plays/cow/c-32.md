Mr. Big rides someone else's wagon: the traveling sensor on
routes the party isn't taking.

### When to use it

- Route intelligence: what happens on the road the party
  didn't choose, inside the caravan the party couldn't
  join, at the waystations between here and the case.

### Mechanics

1. Insertion is charm: teamsters adopt mascots (a toy tied
   to the lead wagon's bow "for luck" — gifted by a
   grateful scholar-passenger at the last town). Caravan
   culture does the rest; nobody audits luck.
2. The brief is a route survey in play 25's grammar: *the
   road; who stops the wagons; where the toll is paid and
   to whom; home when the wagons reach the river.* The
   product: chokepoints, shakedowns, the "bandits" who
   somehow know schedules, the waystation keeper who signals
   ahead.
3. The recovery meets the caravan at its destination — Joe
   the passenger's colleague, come to collect the luck-toy
   with thanks and a coin for the teamster — and the road's
   whole story debriefs into the journal.
4. The rail: caravans get *hit* — the standing *if* is
   pre-loaded (*if fire: fly home now*, play 25), because a
   mascot's luck is not a doctrine and 1 HP rides every
   mile of this play.

### At the table

Mechanically: "We can't ride the north road and watch the
south one — so the south road gets the mascot. Mr. Big
ties to Harl's lead wagon for luck at dawn. The brief:
who stops the wagons, who takes money, home at the river.
When Harl's caravan gets 'lucky' past the toll gorge that
robs everyone else, we'll know exactly what that luck
cost and who sold it."

Verbally: *"The teamsters swear by the little cow on the
lead bow before the trip is half done — the road's been
kind, the tolls polite. None of them will ever know the
luck was mutual: the road has never in its life been so
thoroughly witnessed."*
