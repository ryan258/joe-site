Toy-mode's foundation con: Mr. Big, motionless on a surface, in
the room where the truth gets said.

### When to use it

- The premium insertion (J-88 wrote the protocol and the hard
  rails; this play is the placement craft): parlors, offices,
  meeting rooms — anywhere a worn toy can plausibly sit.

### Mechanics

1. Placement is provenance: a toy needs a *reason* — the
   client's child left it (play 28's setup), Joe "forgot" it
   (J-88's classic), it arrived in a confiscated bag (play
   31), it's simply been there so long nobody remembers (the
   long con, play 39). The reason is chosen before the toy is.
2. Surface craft: shelf height beats table height (eyes pass
   over shelves; hands clear tables), sightline to the room's
   *working* zone (desk, hearth chairs) beats the decorative
   zone, and dust-adjacency sells tenure.
3. The watch itself runs plays 17/19/24 from a fixed point:
   feelings always, framed looks on pings, the log after.
   Stillness is absolute while observed — a Fey spirit's
   stillness, the sheet's one flawless disguise.
4. J-88's rails repeat because they're load-bearing: never
   where *Detect Magic* sweeps are routine, never past the
   agreed hour, never twice on a suspicious host — and the
   abort image is pre-loaded before placement, every time.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The magistrate's parlor shelf, between the
dented tankard and the dusty regimental miniature — tenure
by association. Provenance: his niece visits Thursdays, and
children leave things. Sightline covers the desk. Abort
image is loaded, sweep-risk is low — his clerk does the
tidying and hates magic. Placement tonight."

Verbally: *"The parlor's shelf holds a man's whole
sentimental museum: the tankard, the toy soldier, and —
since Thursday, though he could not say which Thursday —
one small worn cow, facing, as it happens, the desk."*
