String, trailed by wing: the physical route-marker through
mazes, caves, and the dark that eats maps.

### When to use it

- Labyrinths (built or grown), cave systems, and any
  route the party must retrace at speed in the dark —
  J-97's retreat script wants a handrail.

### Mechanics

1. The mechanism: a spool's free end tied at the
   entrance, the spool riding Cow's talons paying out
   along the chosen route — ounces of thread covering
   hundreds of feet of route (twine for tenure, silk
   thread for stealth; the spool is a copper's worth of
   J-148's shopping list).
2. Cow's advantage over the walking version: the thread
   lays *above* the floor's traffic (rafter-height,
   ledge-height — briefed by image: *high, along the
   left*), invisible to floor-level eyes and pests, and
   the layer never walks into what the maze keeps
   (plays 5, 23 scout as the thread lays).
3. The retrace protocol: hand over hand at speed, in
   the dark, no Investigation rolls, no debate at
   junctions — the thread converts J-97's worst case
   (retreat, lightless, pursued) into a procedure.
4. The counter-use is briefed too: threads can be
   *followed* both ways — hostile country gets the
   recovery variant (Cow re-spools the thread behind
   the party's final exit, and the maze keeps no
   record anyone passed).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we go into that warren: thread's
tied at the arch, Cow lays it at ledge-height along
our route as we move. If this goes wrong, the order
is 'hands on the thread, run' — no map-reading in the
dark, no votes at junctions. And Cow re-spools it on
our way out; the warren doesn't get to remember us."

Verbally: *"The maze has eaten better parties: it
holds their bones at its junctions like arguments.
This party walks in under a silver thread laid at
shoulder height by patient wings — and when the dark
finally lunges, they leave hand over hand at a dead
run, unspooling backward out of the labyrinth's
memory."*
