Retreat overwatch: the eyes that face backward so the
running party doesn't have to.

### When to use it

- Every withdrawal (J-97 runs the script; this is its
  air support): pursued, pressed, or merely prudent —
  the party moving away from contact is blind exactly
  where the danger is.

### Mechanics

1. Station: above and *behind* the party's line of
   retreat — the pursuit's country, not the party's —
   reporting pursuit state in the codebook's tersest
   grammar: *rain-smell* (they follow), the count,
   *still water* (they've stopped), and the priceless
   *stone* (they've stopped *and set an ambush ahead
   of you* — pursuers who know the ground cut
   corners; the rear guard eye watches for the
   pursuit that becomes a front).
2. Joe's rearguard spells (J-97's frost, silk, and
   grease) now land on *intelligence* instead of
   guesses: the Web goes across the path the pursuit
   is actually on, the Ray of Frost takes the runner
   who's actually gaining — every slowing effect
   placed by the eye that sees the whole chase.
3. The rally-point clearance is the last leg (J-97's
   final clause): Cow sweeps the fallback *before*
   the party arrives — the retreat that ends in a
   second ambush is the genre's oldest trick, and a
   thirty-second flyover retires it.
4. Cow retreats *last* and highest: the rear guard
   eye disengages upward when the party's clear —
   the one member of the column no pursuit on legs
   can catch.

### At the table

Mechanically: "We're leaving — script's running, and
Cow takes the back door: high, behind us, over their
country. ...Ping: rain-smell, three, gaining on the
left path. So the Web goes across the *left* path —
thank you — and... stone. They've stopped and
they're cutting toward the ford. The ford's no
longer our route. Rally point two, and Cow sweeps
it first."

Verbally: *"The pursuit is fast, local, and clever —
it knows the ford, the shortcut, the perfect place
to be waiting. It has simply never chased anyone
whose hindsight was literal: sixty feet up,
facing backward, and narrating its every clever
move to the people it's failing to surprise."*
