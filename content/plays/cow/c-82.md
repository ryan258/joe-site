Cow and the beasts: the Fey presence as the party's channel
to everything on four legs.

### When to use it

- Guard dogs, wary horses, the hermit's bear, the
  wolf-pack winter — encounters where the obstacle or
  the witness is an animal, and the party's words are
  noise.

### Mechanics

1. The premise, honestly DM-mediated throughout: the
   sheet makes Cow a *Fey spirit* — and folklore's
   whole testimony is that beasts know the Fair
   Folk and mind them. The mechanical floor is the
   calming effect (play 70's picket-line rapport);
   the ceiling — crude exchange with animals
   (impressions, warnings, the dog's memory of the
   intruder) — is the DM's gift to ask for, not
   assume.
2. The guard-dog problem (infiltration's classic)
   gets the diplomatic option: Cow's settled
   presence at the kennel *before* the approach —
   dogs that don't alarm at what the old spirit has
   vouched for (DM's ruling; the fallback stays
   mundane: the sausage, the wind's direction, the
   route the dogs don't cover — plays 1 and 9 found
   it).
3. The witness variant treats beasts as memory: the
   dead man's dog *saw the killer* — Cow's crude
   channel (the dog's fear-memory as impression:
   its shape, its smell) is fragmentary evidence
   per the sheet's own rule, nominated to the file
   like every sonar read (J-79 governs beasts too).
4. The relationship compounds like play 143's
   garden: the party's mounts, the inn's cat, the
   farm dogs on the regular route — a standing
   gentleness (the Keeper's ethic, extended) that
   pays off in the hundred small ways animals ease
   or obstruct a traveling life.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Two mastiffs on that yard, and I'd
rather not meet them — Cow spends the evening at
the kennel fence first, just... being old and
calm at them. DM's call what that buys. If
they're settled by moonrise, we walk the east
wall; if not, we're back to the sausage and the
wind plan."

Verbally: *"The mastiffs have ended eleven
burglaries — they are famous, and fed
accordingly. Tonight they lie in strange calm,
watching the wall-walkers pass, because
something at the fence all evening has been
radiating, in the oldest language there is:
these ones are known to me, and you have done
your work well."*
