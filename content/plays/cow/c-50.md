Dropped powder: marking, revealing, and spoiling — the
airborne applications of a copper's worth of dust.

### When to use it

- The invisible enemy needs outlining (J-101), the dark
  water needs a current-read, the mark needs marking —
  jobs a talon-load of loose material solves from above.

### Mechanics

1. The legal line, drawn honestly: Cow **cannot
   attack** — dropping something *to harm* is out. But
   releasing a pouch of flour over a *square* (marking
   an area, not striking a creature) is the DM's
   ruling to make, and most tables allow the
   revelation play: powder settling *around* an unseen
   thing outlines it for everyone (J-101's flush,
   airborne, no slot).
2. The marking applications are uncontroversial: chalk
   dust on the roof of the safehouse (visible from the
   party's vantage, invisible from the street), flour
   on the night trail at the fork the party must find
   at speed, powdered marker into the stream to read
   where the culvert surfaces (play 10's current-
   question, answered in white).
3. The spoiler variant serves the retreat: pepper or
   soot into the kennel yard as the party breaks
   contact — hounds tracking by scent meet a nose full
   of static (DM adjudicates; J-97's script will take
   any rounds it can get).
4. Load and rigging: an open-mouth pouch, talon-
   carried, released by letting go — ounces, one use,
   rigged in camp (J-148's shopping list gains a line:
   flour, pepper, chalk).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Something invisible crossed the flour
line — there. Cow's overhead with the second pouch:
release over that square... it's outlined, everyone —
shoulders and a blade, moving left. It's not
invisible anymore; it's a silhouette with a
schedule."

Verbally: *"The assassin's finest spell meets a
pouch of kitchen flour released from forty feet, and
the terrible unseen thing becomes, in one soft white
breath, a man-shaped pastry that every archer on the
wall can price."*
