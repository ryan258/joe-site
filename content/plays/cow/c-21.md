Moving operations inside a 100-foot bubble: the footwork that
keeps the link live while both ends travel.

### When to use it

- Mobile jobs where live coverage matters: tails (J-138),
  escorted infiltrations, street crossings under overwatch —
  anything where Cow's real-time feed beats fragments.

### Mechanics

1. The bubble is Joe-centered and 100 feet: Cow's flying
   coverage of a *moving* Joe is a leapfrog — perch ahead on
   the route, cover Joe's transit past, lift and re-perch
   ahead — the link never breaks and the bird never orbits
   (orbiting birds get noticed; perched ones don't).
2. Route planning is bubble planning: Joe's path chosen for
   perch density (eaves, posts, trees at sub-100-foot
   spacing) the way infantry chooses cover — a boulevard of
   bare cobbles is a link-dead zone; plan around it or accept
   fragments across it.
3. The handoff rhythm is codebook-light: *still water* on each
   re-perch (clear ahead), silence during lifts — Joe reads
   the link's rhythm like a pulse without spending attention.
4. Vertical counts: 100 feet is a sphere — Cow at 80 feet up
   covers Joe at street level with 60 feet of lateral slack,
   and altitude perches (spires, gables) stretch one perch
   over two blocks.

### At the table

Mechanically: "My route to the drop runs the arcade — perch
posts every sixty feet, so Cow leapfrogs me: perch, cover my
stretch, lift, re-perch. Still-water pings at each hop. The
plaza's the dead zone — forty seconds of fragments — so
that's where I'll walk fastest and look most boring."

Verbally: *"The scholar strolls the arcade at a reading
pace, and above him — always slightly ahead, always
somewhere a bird would sit anyway — his second pair of eyes
keeps the whole street on a silent, rolling guarantee."*
