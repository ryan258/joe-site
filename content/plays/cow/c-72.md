Cow shuttling between divided halves of the party: the
courier-loop that keeps two operations one operation.

### When to use it

- Deliberate splits past all channels (Message's 120,
  the link's 100): the two-site job, the divided
  approach, the team left holding the boat — J-22's
  relay chain when even the chain won't stretch.

### Mechanics

1. The loop's design: a scheduled circuit (Cow flies
   team A → team B → home on agreed marks — the
   bell, the moonrise, the hour-glass) carrying
   note-drops both ways (play 42's postal service on
   a timetable) — each team gets the other's status
   at loop-frequency, and *silence on the loop* is
   itself the signal (a missed circuit means
   trouble at one end: pre-briefed, J-75's dead-man
   logic).
2. Frequency prices against exposure: dense loops
   (quarter-hourly) for hot operations, sparse
   (dawn/dusk) for watches measured in days — each
   transit is sky-time (the audit rides every leg),
   so the loop runs at the *minimum* frequency the
   operation's decisions actually need.
3. The bridge carries *command*, not just news:
   Joe's team holds the decision authority
   (pre-agreed), and the loop's notes carry
   go/no-go/abort in the party's word-code —
   the split operation keeps one brain at
   loop-latency.
4. Emergency override: either team's flare-equivalent
   (the agreed *visible* signal — play 52's flag
   grammar works ground-to-air too: the cloak on
   the rock) pulls Cow off-schedule to that end —
   the loop is a timetable with an interrupt, not
   a prison.

### At the table

Mechanically: "We split at the bridge: Tam's team
takes the mill, we take the manor, and Cow runs
the loop on the half-hour — notes both ways, my
team holds go/no-go. If a circuit comes up empty,
the other team moves to the rally *immediately* —
missed loop means someone's in trouble. Cloak on
the wall pulls the bird off-schedule. Questions?"

Verbally: *"Two teams work a mile apart in
hostile dark, and between them swings a silent
half-hourly pendulum — notes tied in reed, a
timetable with wings — so that when the mill
team's third circuit comes back empty, the
manor team is moving before the echo of the
nothing arrives."*
