The hidden knowledge itself: the protocol for asking Cow what
Cow remembers.

### When to use it

- The campaign's deep questions — the toy's origin, the
  spirit's old enemies, the knowledge others would coerce
  (the sheet's words) — asked rarely, carefully, and on
  the spirit's terms.

### Mechanics

1. The canon governs absolutely: Cow's complex memories
   are **fragmentary clues, controlled or approved by
   the DM** — the archive is real, ancient, and not a
   database. The protocol exists *because* the resource
   is precious and bounded: bad asking wastes it; good
   asking is a campaign engine.
2. Good asking is Joe's own craft turned inward (play
   19's framing discipline): one concrete anchor per
   consultation — an object in hand, a name spoken, a
   place stood in — beats every open question ever
   posed ("what do you remember?" returns fog; "have
   you seen this seal before?" returns a fragment
   worth ink).
3. The fragments are processed like any evidence
   (J-76's chain): inked immediately, dated, cross-
   referenced against the mundane record (J-141's
   archives corroborate or complicate — a spirit's
   memory of a place two centuries gone meets the
   land-registry's version, and the *differences*
   are the case).
4. The consent rail is the whole character: Joe asks;
   he never presses (the sheet's backstory — he
   treated the spirit as a person, and the bond
   exists *because* of that). A consultation Cow
   declines is closed, logged as declined, and its
   subject flagged — the decline is itself a datum
   (something there is heavy), and the Keeper's
   patience is the price of the archive's trust.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The seal from the vault matches
nothing in three archives — so tonight, the other
archive. One anchor, one question: I hold the seal
where Cow can see it. 'Before?' ...A fragment: cold
stone, this mark on a door, the feeling of being
*carried past it in a pocket*. That's pre-Mr.-Big.
Inked, dated, flagged — and I don't ask again
tonight."

Verbally: *"Joe holds the wax seal up in the
firelight, and asks the way he always asks: once,
gently, with the question already resigned to any
answer. The little cow is very still. Then,
across the link, something older than the
kingdom's name hands him one cold image — a door,
this mark, long ago — and closes, carefully, like
a book being saved from a clumsy reader."*
