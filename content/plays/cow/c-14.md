Aerial tracking: fleeing quarry, wagon dust, and the trails that
ground-level eyes lose at the first stream.

### When to use it

- Pursuit: the ambusher who ran, the kidnapper's cart, the
  stampeded horses — anything that must be followed *now*,
  across country, faster than tracks can be read on foot.

### Mechanics

1. Altitude defeats tracking's classic failures: streams,
   rock-fields, and backtrails hide prints from the ground and
   nothing from a column of dust, a wake of birds, a line of
   swaying crops seen from 200 feet.
2. Division of labor: Cow *maintains contact* (60 fly matches
   horses; Flyby means even a noticed owl is never caught);
   the party *follows Cow* — link-range relay where terrain
   allows (play 22), landmark-fragments where it doesn't
   ("the burned oak, then the ford" — play 25's advance
   grammar).
3. Contact discipline: the quarry that stops is more
   interesting than the quarry that runs — stopping means
   destination, meeting, or ambush, and the difference is the
   next hour of everyone's life. Cow's brief: on a stop,
   *perch and count* (play 3) before the party closes.
4. The ground team still cuts sign at checkpoints (+10 on
   what the quarry dropped, dragged, or bled — J-76): air
   holds the thread, ground reads the beads.

### At the table

Mechanically: "They've got two minutes and a river on us —
which is nothing: Cow's already got their dust. We follow the
bird at a canter. When they stop, Cow perches and counts
before we blunder in — a halt this early means they're either
home or waiting for us."

Verbally: *"The raiders ride hard through every trick the
country offers — the ford, the shale, the doubled trail —
and the sky follows them anyway, patient as a bill coming
due."*
