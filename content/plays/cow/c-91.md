What Cow's reactions to *names* imply: the spoken-word
seismograph, and the leads it hands the case.

### When to use it

- Interviews, negotiations, tavern talk — any scene
  where names are said aloud (and the campaign's own
  deep file: names connected to the toy's origin and
  the spirit's past, J-100's counter-file).

### Mechanics

1. The instrument: names carry freight for a spirit
   with centuries of ledger — a name said in Cow's
   hearing that lands (the flinch across the link,
   the sudden attention, the old grief) is a
   *connection* between the speaker's subject and
   the spirit's past, arriving as feeling
   (fragmentary, DM-curated, the canon's rule as
   always).
2. The protocol is passive by design: no
   interrogation-by-name-list (reciting names *at*
   Cow is play 87's bad asking, and the archive
   closes to it) — the ledger collects *incidental*
   reactions: the interview where a patron's
   grandfather gets mentioned and the link goes
   cold; the ballad whose third verse makes the
   toy in the satchel go *heavy*. Logged, dated,
   never chased in the moment.
3. The processing braid (the file's standard):
   name-reactions cross-referenced against J-141's
   archives and J-78's histories — a name that
   moves both the spirit *and* the record is a
   campaign thread; a name that moves only the
   spirit is a thread the *record lost*, which is
   better (the mistranslated sources of Joe's own
   backstory live exactly there — Goblin and Orc
   texts included, J-78's languages earning their
   keep).
4. The gravest entries invert the flow: names that
   make Cow *afraid* go straight to the
   counter-file (J-100) — the spirit's old
   creditors are the campaign's late-game cast,
   and the ledger's fear-column is the party's
   earliest warning system for who is eventually
   coming.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The bard's border-ballad tonight —
third verse names a 'Lord of the Counting Mist,'
and the link went cold as well-water. Not asking
Cow anything; just inking it: name, source, date,
feeling. Cross-reference tomorrow — and if the
archives have never heard of him, that's not
nothing, that's *better*. The record forgot
someone the spirit hasn't."

Verbally: *"The tavern applauds the old ballad
and calls for another. In the corner, a scholar
who appeared to be dozing writes seven words in
a book too small to read across a table — and
in his satchel, something that flinched at the
third verse settles slowly, grateful, as ever,
that its Keeper never asks out loud."*
