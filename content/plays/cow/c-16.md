The link carries emotions and images, not sentences. The codebook
turns that channel into a language with vocabulary drills behind it.

### When to use it

- Continuously — every play in this file runs on it. Built in
  downtime (play 95), spoken in the field.

### Mechanics

1. The channel's native grammar (per the sheet): **emotions,
   images, remembered sensations, intentions** — so the codebook
   assigns meanings to *rememberable* things: *fire* = urgent,
   *still water* = all clear, *stone* = hold position, *the smell
   of rain* = enemies moving, *Joe's own face* = come home.
2. Numbers travel as repetition or as remembered groups (*the
   four cups on the shelf* = four); direction as landmarks, never
   as compass words; time as light (*dawn-colored* = soon after
   dawn).
3. Two-way discipline: Joe's outbound commands use the same
   vocabulary — a shared codebook halves the ambiguity in both
   directions, and ambiguity is the only failure mode this
   channel has.
4. The codebook is written down (the journal's back page, J-83's
   ink rule) — not because Cow forgets, but because *Joe* under
   pressure reaches for words, and the page retrains him faster
   than the field does.

### At the table

Mechanically: "For the table's reference, our standing code:
fire is urgent, still water is clear, stone is hold, rain-smell
is movement toward us, and my face means return. When I say
'Cow sends rain-smell and the four cups,' that's four hostiles
inbound, and I knew it in the time it takes to feel something."

Verbally: *"They speak in borrowed weather and remembered
kitchens — a language with no words, no sound, and no way to be
overheard, fluent after years in exactly two speakers in all
the world."*
