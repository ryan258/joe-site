Antidotes, potions, and medicine, delivered at 60 feet per
round to whoever is dying too far away.

### When to use it

- The poisoned ally across the battlefield, the sick child
  in the quarantined house, the potion the trapped scout
  needs *now* — ounces of glass where minutes are the
  whole question.

### Mechanics

1. Load reality: a small vial rides the talon-carry inside
   the DM's ounces (a full potion bottle is the boundary
   case — decant to a vial when the mission's known;
   J-146's bench keeps emergency doses in flight-weight
   glass by policy).
2. In combat, the run is a Flyby lane (play 41's
   technique under fire): altitude approach, one dropping
   pass to the recipient's hands or lap, exit climbing —
   Cow spends the minimum rounds in the AoE-and-arrow
   layer (the 1 HP constitution prices every leg; J-89's
   no-fly call can veto mid-mission via the *fire* abort).
3. The recipient drinks it themselves (Bonus Action,
   J-68) — Cow delivers, never administers (no hands; the
   unconscious-ally case needs a person, and the vial
   flight's job becomes reaching the *person nearest* the
   fallen).
4. The quarantine variant is the play at its best: sealed
   doors, watched windows, and medicine that arrives
   nightly by the one route quarantines never close —
   with play 17's sonar reading the house's despair on
   every visit, which is how Joe knows when it turns.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Senna's poisoned on the far tower and
the stairs are held — vial's decanted and flying: over
the melee at sixty feet, one dropping pass to her lap,
climb out the far side. Two rounds exposed. Senna, it's
coming from the sky — Bonus Action when it lands."

Verbally: *"Between the poison and the antidote stands
a held staircase, forty raiders, and a locked hall —
every route a soldier would count. The medicine takes
the route no one holds, cradled under silent wings,
and lands in her lap like a small impossible mercy."*
