Watching the party's own back: Cow as the standing answer to
"are we being followed?"

### When to use it

- Cities and roads where the party is *interesting*:
  after loud jobs, during sensitive cases, always once
  the sheet's stalkers (J-100) are known active — the
  play runs on a schedule, not on paranoia's mood.

### Mechanics

1. The mechanism inverts the tail (J-138, play 14):
   Cow trails *the party* at altitude and watches what
   the party's wake does — the figure who turns when
   they turn, the loiterer who leapfrogs, the beggar
   who's moved three corners in an hour (surveillance
   has a shape, and from 100 feet up the shape is
   legible).
2. The scheduled sweep beats the reactive one: a
   standing pattern (Cow takes the back-trail for the
   first ten minutes of every departure; one full
   route-audit per day in cities) catches the tail
   *early* — and early is when counter-surveillance
   is cheap (route changes and boring days, not
   confrontations).
3. The confirmed tail forks three ways, Joe's call:
   **starve it** (boring routes until it withdraws —
   cheapest); **follow it home** (the tail becomes
   the lead: Cow takes the tail's own departure,
   play 58's logic — who watches the watchers is
   suddenly answerable); **feed it** (the tail
   reports what the party *shows* it — J-15's
   phantom theater has an audience now, and the
   misdirection writes itself).
4. The link discipline on the move: play 21's
   leapfrog keeps the counter-tail inside live range
   on planned routes; fragments cover the rest —
   and the *fire*-ping means the tail closed to
   contact range, which is no longer surveillance
   (J-114's stack stands up).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Standing order while we're in this
city: Cow audits our back-trail the first ten
minutes of every departure. ...And there it is,
day two: grey cap, three corners, leapfrogging.
We starve him today — market, temple, dinner,
crushingly boring — and tonight Cow follows *him*
home. By tomorrow we'll know who's paying for
eyes on us, and they still won't know we noticed."

Verbally: *"The watcher is good: unhurried,
forgettable, never twice in the same coat. He
watches the party for two days and learns their
laundry, their suppers, their tedium. It does
not occur to him, even once, to price the small
bird that has attended his every corner — or
to wonder why, on the third morning, the
scholar's route walks past his own front door."*
