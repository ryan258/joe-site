Ending surprise as a concept: the next room, read before the
party's boots commit to it.

### When to use it

- Dungeon flow: every door, stairhead, and corridor mouth
  in hostile structure — the play that runs between
  fights so fights start on the party's terms.

### Mechanics

1. The full stack in sequence (assembled from parts):
   pre-entry feel at the gap (play 23 — occupied or
   not, and *how*), the interior frames where gaps
   allow sight (play 6's craft at keyhole scale), and
   the transom entry for full survey when the room's
   worth it (play 44's navigation rule: fly known
   rooms).
2. The product standardized to the party's three
   stack-options (J-136's door doctrine): *still
   water* — enter working; *hearth-warm* — enter
   ready; *held breath* — the room gets J-16's remote
   open and J-2's silk instead of anyone's body.
3. Initiative economics, stated plainly: a fight the
   party starts on *its* terms — positioned, buffed,
   the Web pre-aimed at the choke — is worth more
   than any spell in the book. The door report is
   how J-96's opening script gets its "read" for
   free, before initiative exists.
4. The pace cost is real and paid consciously:
   thirty seconds per door is an hour per level —
   hostile-structure standard, relaxed only when the
   clock (the ritual upstairs, the drowning prisoner)
   outranks the ambush risk. Say which mode the party
   is in; drift between them is how doors win.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Door drill, every door, standard pace —
we're not on a clock tonight. This one: held-breath,
two of them, left side by the feel. So: Mage Hand
opens from the right, Web's pre-aimed at the left
interior, and we enter a room that thought it was
ambushing somebody."

Verbally: *"The dungeon's whole strategy is doors:
each one a held breath, a drawn blade, a purchased
surprise. The party walks its halls opening them like
mail from an old friend — every ambush interviewed,
priced, and pre-empted by thirty patient seconds of
attention at the gap."*
