Getting Mr. Big taken: the insertion that rides the enemy's own
procedures into their strong room.

### When to use it

- Targets that search visitors and impound belongings: prisons,
  audiences with paranoid nobility, guildhalls with door
  discipline — places where *being taken* is the only way in.

### Mechanics

1. The physics: confiscated goods flow to predictable rooms —
   the gatehouse chest, the steward's cabinet, the evidence
   shelf — which are, by construction, *inside* the security
   perimeter and adjacent to the interesting conversations.
2. The setup: Mr. Big rides visibly among Joe's effects
   (sentimental, worthless, unremarkable — exactly what
   searches wave through *or* impound whole); Joe's mild
   protest ("it was a gift — do be careful with it") sells
   value-to-owner and buys placement-with-care.
3. Inside, the standard watch stack runs (plays 17, 19, 24)
   from wherever the procedure shelved it — and confiscation
   rooms hear things: guards gossip over the impound chest,
   stewards count seizures aloud.
4. Retrieval is built in: confiscated goods get *returned* —
   Joe collects Mr. Big and its memories at the exit, or the
   "lost" toy justifies a return visit (with paperwork; play
   84's forged claim-chit exists for a reason). The one rail:
   never gambit a facility that *destroys* contraband — the
   provenance research (play 26) is not optional.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The keep impounds everything at the gate —
which is the plan. Mr. Big goes in my satchel, I make the
right small fuss, and the toy spends our whole audience in
the gatehouse chest, listening to the garrison's unguarded
opinions. We collect it, and everything it heard, on the
way out."

Verbally: *"The sergeant tosses the shabby toy into the
impound chest and forgets it before the lid falls. For the
next three hours, the gatehouse — where soldiers say what
they actually think — entertains its most attentive guest
in years."*
