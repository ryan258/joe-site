Cow's reaction to objects and workings: the second opinion
on everything magical the party touches.

### When to use it

- Found magic items, active workings, warded thresholds —
  alongside *Detect Magic* and Arcana (J-29, J-77): the
  school and the theory from the spells, the *texture*
  from the spirit.

### Mechanics

1. The instrument's niche: *Detect Magic* reports
   school; Arcana reports theory; Cow reports
   **character** — the sword that reads as *sad*, the
   ward that reads as *hungry*, the amulet whose
   feeling doesn't match its face (a Necromancy
   working dressed in Abjuration's aura is exactly
   the mismatch the three-instrument braid catches
   and no single check does).
2. The protocol is one beat long and standing (the
   pre-touch ritual, J-140's order amended): sweep,
   theory, *resonance* — Cow's read taken before
   hands, logged with the item (play 86's ledger
   has an objects section), and a *revulsion* read
   is a hard pause (not a veto — a demand for the
   long look: some things read wrong because they
   are old and sorrowful; some because they are
   traps; the difference is worth a day of J-141).
3. The cursed-item case is the play's paycheck:
   curses hide from *Identify*-tier magic by
   design (the DM's oldest trick) — but the
   *feeling* of a thing that wants to be picked
   up is exactly what a Fey sense is for
   (folklore again, load-bearing again), and
   "Cow won't perch near it" has saved more
   parties than any abjuration.
4. The rail: resonance nominates, never convicts
   (J-79's rule, for objects) — and Cow is never
   *required* to approach what it recoils from
   (the Keeper doesn't spend the instrument's
   trust on confirming what the recoil already
   said; play 87's consent logic, extended to
   dread).

### At the table

Mechanically: "The circlet detects as Abjuration
and Arcana likes it — but the standing beat:
resonance check. ...Cow won't come within ten
feet of it. That's the mismatch: something
dressed politely. Nobody wears it; it wraps in
lead cloth and rides the outside of the pack
until three archives and a temple have had
opinions. The sword, for the record, reads as
*sad* — that one's just old. It can come."

Verbally: *"The circlet gleams on the velvet,
detected, identified, and approved by every
learned instrument the party owns. The oldest
instrument in the room takes one look and
removes itself to the rafters — and the party,
who have learned exactly what that means, pack
the beautiful thing in lead and gloves, like
the polite lie it is."*
