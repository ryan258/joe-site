Travel days are a resource state, not dead time: watch math,
ritual scheduling, and the marching version of every standing
order.

### When to use it

- Multi-day overland movement — the default state of a
  campaign, run on procedure so the ambush day finds the
  party rich.

### Mechanics

1. **The slot posture**: travel days start *full* — which
   means Joe's rest-day economics invert: rituals do all
   routine work (Detect Magic on the odd find, Alarm at every
   halt — plays 29, 31; zero slots) and the eleven slots ride
   as ambush insurance. A wizard arriving at trouble with
   full slots is the doctrine's whole product.
2. **Watch math with the party's actual numbers**: Joe (+3
   Perception) takes the *middle* watch with Cow's
   Darkvision-120 doing the seeing (play 86's pairing) —
   never the dawn watch (his Mage Armor timing wants the
   morning cast, play 34) and never solo. The high-Wisdom
   allies bracket the night's ends.
3. **The march order is play 136 at road scale**: Cow flies
   point-loose circuits at the hour (the sky's cheap — play
   5), Joe center, and the day's brief covers the two
   pre-decided verbs: *scatter pattern* on "Fireball
   incoming!" (spacing, play 136) and *rally square* on
   contact (the wagon, the oak — named at breakfast, play
   97's fallback discipline).
4. **Evening routine compounds** (the dawn checklist's
   sibling, play 70): Alarm before dinner, camp *inside* its
   cube, tomorrow's route sketched from Cow's last circuit
   while light holds, and the journal's day-page inked —
   because on the road, yesterday's odd rider and today's
   cold campfire are the case files of the week after
   (play 122's tracking eye, ridden daily).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Road rules, same as always: rituals do the
day's magic and my slots sleep full. Cow circuits at each
hour-mark, halts get Alarmed before anyone unbuckles, I
take mid-watch with the bird's eyes, and the words are
'scatter' and 'the oak.' Ask me for the fancy magic on the
day something goes wrong — that's what we're saving it
for."

Verbally: *"Nothing happens for six days, which is to say:
the Alarm learns four false dawns, the journal learns every
cold fire within a mile of the road, and the ambush on the
seventh day finds a wizard with every slot he owns and a
map of exactly where it was always going to happen."*
