The spellbook is a portfolio. Buy well, copy cheap, and never
pay retail for what Savant gives free.

### When to use it

- Every captured spellbook, purchased scroll, and friendly
  wizard — downtime's standing shopping run.

### Mechanics

1. The price sheet: copying a found spell runs **2 hours and
   50 GP per spell level** (ink and practice); the *free*
   channels are the two level-up picks and Savant's Evocation
   dividend (play 75) — so gold buys **non-Evocation utility**
   first (the schools Savant never subsidizes: the *Misty
   Step*s and *Counterspell*s of the future), and Evocation
   almost never (the subclass pays for those).
2. **Captured spellbooks are treasure that compounds**: an
   enemy wizard's book at five copyable spells is worth more
   than most art objects the party haggles over — Joe bids
   accordingly at loot-split, and Arcana (+7) appraises the
   contents *before* the split (quietly).
3. Ritual-tagged spells jump the queue (play 72's shelf grows
   free casting forever): a ritual in the book is a permanent
   zero-slot capability — price *Comprehend Languages*-shaped
   finds (a ritual translator, for *this* investigator) above
   their level.
4. Scrolls fork two ways: **copy it** (consumes the scroll,
   permanent) or **cast it** (once, at need) — copy when it's
   list-worthy forever, hold when it's an emergency-shaped
   one-off. A *Revivify* scroll is insurance; a *Misty Step*
   scroll is a photocopy waiting to happen... a copy candidate.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The hedge-mage's book has five I can use —
that's four hundred gold of copying value, so it comes out of
my loot share happily. Priority order: the ritual first, it
casts free forever; the two utility picks next; and nobody
pay for Evocation — my subclass has a standing donation
program."

Verbally: *"The party weighs the dead wizard's rings and coin.
Joe weighs the book — and by the arithmetic only he is
running, walks away from the split convinced, correctly, that
he robbed everyone."*
