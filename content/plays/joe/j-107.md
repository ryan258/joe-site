Force damage and the un-poisonable, un-frightenable wizard's toolkit.

### When to use it

- Golems, animated armor, guardians — things immune to the party's
  poison, psychic tricks, and intimidation.

### Mechanics

1. Constructs shrug what Joe never had (poison, charm, fear) and
   commonly resist or ignore a lot else — but **force damage is the
   least-resisted type in the game**, and the wand's *Magic Missile*
   is pure force with no attack roll (plays 64–65). Against magic-
   resistant constructs with high AC, the wand is often the party's
   only reliable line.
2. Arcana first, always (play 77): golem types carry famous riders
   (some heal from fire, some slow from cold, some are immune to
   any spell below a threshold). One check redirects the whole
   party's spend.
3. Constructs follow **orders**, and orders have authors:
   Investigation (+10) on the patrol pattern — what it guards, what
   it ignores, where it turns — often yields a *route past* worth
   more than the fight (the maker's instructions are a document, and
   Joe reads documents).
4. *Dispel Magic* on the animating magic is table-dependent (most
   constructs aren't "a spell") — but on the *control amulet*, the
   glyph, the command circle? That's an effect, and play 52 applies.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Iron sentinel — Arcana... it heals from fire, so
everyone holster that. Wand: force darts, no roll against that AC.
And before we spend more: it's walked the same lozenge for ten
minutes — Investigation on the pattern. It guards the door, not the
window."

Verbally: *"The golem cannot be charmed, frightened, poisoned, or
reasoned with. Neither, in the relevant sense, can Joe."*
