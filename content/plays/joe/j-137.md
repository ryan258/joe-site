Extraction with a non-combatant attached: the whole kit,
re-weighted around someone with 4 HP and no saves to speak of.

### When to use it

- Any escort segment: freed prisoners, witnesses (play 37's
  person, now in a dungeon), the client's kid, the wounded.

### Mechanics

1. The principal's station is fixed: **touching-distance of
   Joe** (Sculpt requires seeing them — adjacency guarantees
   it always, play 136's sleeve rule), which converts every
   Joe AoE from "carefully aimed around the civilian" to
   "civilian categorically exempt."
2. Their defensive stack is Joe's charity: *Mage Armor* on the
   unarmored principal (8 hours, play 34's ally clause) is the
   best 1st-level slot of the escort day; the potion rides in
   *their* pocket (they can drink Bonus-Action too; Joe may be
   busy).
3. Route doctrine compresses: no open-ground crossings without
   the full stack (play 126), doors get the welcome-mat
   treatment *behind* the party now (Web the pursuit's side —
   plays 26, 97), and Cow flies *rear*-watch, not forward — the
   escort's threat model is the follow.
4. The hard conversation happens at the door, not under fire:
   "if it goes loud: you hold my sleeve, you do not run — the
   fire will never touch you, the silk isn't for you, and if I
   say *door*, we are both suddenly somewhere else" (*Dimension
   Door*'s passenger clause, play 61, has had their name on it
   since the briefing — play 73's parked 4th).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we move — listen once: your station is my
left sleeve, always. You're wearing my Mage Armor, the potion's
in your pocket and it drinks fast, and every terrible thing I
throw is legally required to miss you — that's not a promise,
it's a school of magic. If I say 'door,' grab on: we exit
through arithmetic."

Verbally: *"The freed scribe flinches at the first Fireball —
and then stares, because the fire has edges, and the edges go
around her like a hem. By the third fight she stands in the
storm calmly, one hand on a grey sleeve, inside the only
weather in the world that reads its guest list."*
