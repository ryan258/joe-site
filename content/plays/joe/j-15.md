The image of a threat, spent as leverage.

### When to use it

- Negotiations and standoffs where the enemy is counting heads.
- Buying one round of hesitation in a fight the party is losing.

### Mechanics

1. Cantrip, Action, 30 feet, 1 minute: a 5-foot-cube image — an armored
   soldier at the doorway, a coiled war-dog in the shadows, a glowing
   sigil on the floor.
2. The image is static-ish (no sound with it — *Minor Illusion* does
   sound **or** image, not both). Place it where stillness is plausible:
   a sniper's silhouette in a window beats a soldier who never blinks.
3. Study action vs DC 15 to discern; interaction reveals.
4. Pair with real party noise elsewhere for a pincer that exists only in
   the enemy's head.

### At the table

Mechanically: "As the bandits size us up, Minor Illusion in the loft
window behind us: a hooded figure with a leveled crossbow. Just the
image, holding still."

Verbally: *"'Count again,' Joe suggests mildly, and the bandit leader's
eyes flick up to the window and find the crossbowman who has been
patient this whole time."*
