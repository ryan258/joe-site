One big enemy, maybe two dangerous friends: the loadout where control
degrades gracefully and every resource stacks on a single problem.

### When to use it

- The legendary-tier single enemy, the duelist-champion, the enemy
  archmage — fights where saves are strong, HP is deep, and Joe's
  save-AoE math weakens.

### Mechanics

1. **Assume saves succeed**: a boss makes DC 15 Dexterity saves more
   often than not — 8d6-for-half is the *pessimistic* plan, not the
   failure case. Attack rolls and auto-hits price better: *Scorching
   Ray* (play 42, swapped in when the target is known) and the wand
   (plays 64–66), which cannot miss at all.
2. **Concentration is the boss's weak organ**: their fight-defining
   spell rides one CON save — the wand's multi-dart save-volley
   (play 66) and *Scorching Ray*'s three instances hammer it;
   *Dispel Magic* (play 52) deletes what's already standing;
   *Counterspell* (play 4) denies the next one. That's the actual
   kill-chain; HP is what the martials are for.
3. **Degrade their action economy**: *Web* still taxes even on
   passed saves (difficult terrain, re-saves on entry); *Grease* +
   frost (play 92) taxes legs; every action the boss spends
   escaping is a round the party attacks and it doesn't.
4. **Joe's survival stack runs the whole fight**: Mirror Image +
   Mage Armor + Shield-policy (play 3), range discipline at 60+
   feet, Cow parked (play 89), Inspiration reserved for the one
   save that ends Joe's participation (play 71). Bosses kill
   wizards who forget they're made of 51 HP.
5. **The 4th is the exit**, not a firework: against something that
   can actually TPK, *Dimension Door* stays parked until it's
   carrying the person who can't walk out (play 97's rule).

questions her concentration must answer one at a time, and silk
across every door she thinks of using."*
