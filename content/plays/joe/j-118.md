Near a drop, every shove is worth more than its dice. The map's
edges are the biggest damage numbers on the field.

### When to use it

- Cliffs, bridges, walkways, towers, pits — any fight where "off"
  is a direction.

### Mechanics

1. The pricing: falling deals **1d6 per 10 feet** (cap 20d6) plus
   Prone plus the climb back — a 60-foot ledge makes *Thunderwave*
   (2d8 + push 10, play 21) into an effective 2d8+6d6 that also
   removes the enemy for rounds. *Gust of Wind*'s repeating
   15-foot push (play 45) turns a walkway into a conveyor.
2. Positioning duty: Joe stands so his cube-and-line spells point
   enemies *at* the edge — the 15-foot Thunderwave cube shoves
   directly away from Joe; where Joe stands decides where "away"
   is. Walk the extra 10 feet; aim the geography.
3. *Grease* at the lip (play 25): Prone at an edge is a save away
   from over it (DM's ruling on the slide — stairs logic, play
   26); enemies *know* it and route around, which is also control.
4. The mirror check, always: everything here is symmetric — Joe
   near a drop is a wand-charge from a 6d6 problem, and *he*
   doesn't bounce. Respect from edges, Misty Step pre-visualized
   (play 38), and never fight on the enemy's chosen balcony.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I'm crossing to the left first — ten feet of
walking so that 'away from me' points off the aqueduct. Now:
Thunderwave. Constitution save DC 15, 2d8, pushed ten — and the
next surface in that direction is the plaza, sixty feet down."

Verbally: *"Joe repositions like a man adjusting a picture frame,
half a degree of intention — and then the aqueduct's railing
learns which three of its guests were never included in the
weight calculations."*
