The same kit, run in reverse. Authorization is just a document, and Joe
makes excellent documents.

### When to use it

- The party needs papers: a gate pass, a transfer order, a customs
  chit, the stand-in ledger for play 81's swap.
- Only with time and a sample — forgery is preparation crystallized;
  a rushed fake is a confession with margins.

### Mechanics

1. Forging with Calligrapher's Supplies: an ability check with the
   tool — typically Dexterity or Intelligence with the supplies
   (proficient: +5 or +7; tables vary — Joe's pitch is Intelligence:
   forgery is knowing what the document *should say*), opposed later
   by the examiner's Investigation.
2. Requirements checklist: an authentic sample, the right paper and
   ink (buy locally — play 78 knows why the watermark matters), the
   seal problem solved (a lifted impression, or the genuine ring from
   play 81), and hours, not minutes.
3. Downstream, the fake is checked at the *gate guard's* passive
   Investigation, not a magistrate's — build documents for their
   actual audience. Boring papers survive; impressive ones get
   escalated.
4. The ethical rail (it's on the sheet): Joe forges access, not ruin —
   passes and permissions, never evidence that convicts a person. The
   Ideal is load-bearing.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I have the customs chit Senna lifted and a quiet
evening. Calligrapher's supplies: I'm producing tomorrow's version for
a cart and four passengers — same clerk's hand, same ink batch,
yesterday's stamp re-inked. Intelligence with tools... 24. It only has
to bore one gate guard for ten seconds."

Verbally: *"By lamplight, Joe writes another man's handwriting with
another man's patience, and the border opens for a wagon that was
always supposed to be there."*
