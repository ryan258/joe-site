The kill-zone crossing: forty exposed feet between covers, and
the stack that makes it survivable.

### When to use it

- Courtyards, killing fields, watched streets, the gap between
  siege lines — anywhere the enemy's ranged threat owns a space
  the party must cross.

### Mechanics

1. Sequence the stack: **Mirror Image before stepping out**
   (play 41 — the d6 eats volleys), **the smoke you can't cast**
   — Joe has no *Fog Cloud*; the poor man's version is Minor
   Illusion's freestanding wall-section (play 14) moving cover
   that draws fire at *zero* — and **Shield held** for the bolt
   that beats the images (play 3).
2. Cross on the enemy's reload rhythm: Cow's overwatch counts
   the volley cycle (play 5) — crossing *after* a volley beats
   crossing into one; the timing brief is worth more than any
   AC.
3. Split the fire economy: crossers move in pairs on opposite
   diagonals — an archer choosing targets is an archer not
   leading either well. Joe crosses in the *second* pair (his
   reaction protects, and dead wizards protect nobody).
4. Suppression is real even in D&D: a *Fireball* into the
   archers' position the round before the crossing (play 110)
   converts overwatch into self-care; even on saves, 4d6 apiece
   rearranges priorities.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow counts a volley every six seconds — so we
move on the fourth beat, right after one lands. Mirror Image
is up, illusory wagon 'parked' mid-yard as cover that doesn't
exist, and the Fireball goes into the gatehouse *first*. Pairs,
diagonals; I cross second with Shield in hand."

Verbally: *"The courtyard is a sentence punctuated by arrows,
and Joe teaches the party to read its rhythm — stepping through
the commas, behind a wagon made of nothing, wearing three
spare selves like punctuation of his own."*
