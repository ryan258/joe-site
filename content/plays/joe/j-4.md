The enemy caster's turn simply doesn't happen.

### When to use it

- Enemy magic is the central threat: their caster's *Fireball*,
  *Hypnotic Pattern*, or a heal that undoes the party's round.
- Hold the reaction judiciously — a round with *Counterspell* available
  is a round the enemy caster is playing scared. Do not burn it on a
  cantrip unless that cantrip is about to drop an ally.
- Budget check: it competes with *Shield* for the same reaction. Against
  a caster, denial usually beats +5 AC.

### Mechanics

1. Reaction when Joe **sees** a creature within 60 feet casting a spell
   with Verbal, Somatic, or Material components. Sight is required —
   hearing a chant from behind a door is not a trigger. It fails
   against invisible, hidden, or Subtle-style casters, and against a
   spell cast with no components at all.
2. Cast *Counterspell* (3rd level). The target makes a **Constitution
   saving throw vs DC 15**. On a failure the spell dissipates with no
   effect and the casting action is wasted; the target's spell slot is
   not expended.
3. Joe spends a 3rd-level slot regardless of the outcome — pick targets
   worth it.
4. *Dispel Magic* is the sibling play for effects already running
   (auto-ends spells of 3rd level or lower; Intelligence check DC
   10 + spell level for higher, at +4).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Reaction — *Counterspell*, 3rd level, at the robed one.
Constitution save against DC 15 or the spell fails."

Verbally: *"Joe watches the incantation the way he reads a ledger —
finds the error in the third syllable and presses one finger against
the air. 'No.' The magic unravels mid-word."*

Tell the DM at the start of caster fights: "I'm holding my reaction for
enemy spellcasting." It sets the trigger cleanly and speeds the table up.
