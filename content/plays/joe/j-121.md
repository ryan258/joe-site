Swamps pre-cast half of Joe's control for free. Spend the savings.

### When to use it

- Marsh, mire, mudflats, deep snow — maps that are already
  difficult terrain before Joe spends anything.

### Mechanics

1. Stack on the terrain's work: enemies already at half speed
   take *Ray of Frost* (-10) toward zero — the standstill combo
   (play 92) runs *without* the Grease slot here; the bog is the
   Grease.
2. Web anchors love wetlands' snags and reed-banks less than
   walls — but mangrove and deadfall country is anchor-rich;
   read anchors like cover (play 2's habit, outdoors).
3. The party's mobility asymmetry is buildable: firm-ground
   routes Cow scouts from above (the dry line reads clearly from
   40 feet up — play 5) let allies move at full speed while
   enemies wade. Fights are won in the route brief.
4. Fire behaves strangely in wet country (soaked fuel, play
   116's rain note) but *marsh gas* behaves memorably — if the
   DM's swamp bubbles, Fire Bolt at 120 feet is how you find
   out from a professional distance (and Gust of Wind, swapped
   in, clears the pocket — play 44).
5. Investigation bonus: mud is a *ledger* — tracks, depth,
   direction, count, hours-old, all Study-action food (+10) that
   dry stone never offers. In bog country, Joe reads the floor
   like correspondence.

### At the table

Mechanically: "They're wading — that's already half speed, so
one Ray of Frost each puts their effective move at a crawl:
no Grease needed, the swamp's donating it. Cow says the firm
line runs left of the dead cypress: we move full speed on it,
they don't, and the range just keeps opening."

Verbally: *"The mire does not take sides, but it takes
notes — and Joe, reading the wet page of it, simply arranges
to be the only party the swamp isn't slowing."*
