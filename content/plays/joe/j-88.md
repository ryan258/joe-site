Cow's masterpiece: the familiar who is *also* a beloved, battered
child's toy — and can hold perfectly still about it.

### When to use it

- Getting a sensor inside places no owl and no wizard enters: a
  suspect's parlor, a negotiation chamber, a child's sickroom, a
  cell Joe is thrown into "with only his sentimental effects."
- Any scene where a toy on a shelf is beneath notice — which is most
  scenes.

### Mechanics

1. The toy-cow appearance is the sheet's canonical cosmetic reskin of
   the Owl familiar: at rest, motionless, Cow **is** a fist-sized worn
   toy to any observer — no check, because nothing about a toy
   invites one. (Moving while observed forfeits the cover; a Fey
   spirit's stillness is very good.)
2. Insertion vectors: Joe "absent-mindedly" leaves Mr. Big on the
   table; the toy is confiscated *into the room Joe wanted watched*;
   a child carries it home. Retrieval is a flight (play 87) or an
   apology-visit.
3. Inside 100 feet, the link streams the room's *feel* and images
   continuously; Joe's Bonus Action to borrow eyes (play 90) gets the
   detail. Beyond 100 feet, Cow observes and *remembers* — debrief on
   return, in fragments the DM curates (the sheet's rule on Cow's
   memories holds for new ones too).
4. The hard rail: this is the single riskiest thing Joe can do,
   because losing Mr. Big is his greatest fear made procedural. The
   Trojan is never played where a mage-checker with *Detect Magic*
   sweeps (Cow reads as a creature/aura — play 29 in enemy hands),
   never left past the agreed hour, and never used twice on the same
   suspicious host. The Keeper protocol (play 100) is the abort
   plan, pre-loaded.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before the guards walk me out, I 'forget' Mr. Big on
the magistrate's side table — Cow holding toy-still. The link's good
through that wall from the plaza: for the next hour I'm getting
everything said in that office. Cow knows the abort image. If anyone
casts so much as a cantrip in there, we're done and Cow flies."

Verbally: *"The magistrate's clerk almost bins the shabby little cow,
then leaves it, as people always do. It sits with its worn face
toward the desk, listening the way only very old toys and very good
scholars know how to listen."*
