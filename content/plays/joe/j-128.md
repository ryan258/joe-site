The 300-foot war: what a single Evoker does to massed forces
before they ever reach "encounter distance."

### When to use it

- Field battles, siege defenses, caravan overwatch — engagements
  that start at bowshot and beyond.

### Mechanics

1. The range ladder is the org chart: **300 ft** — *Ice Storm*
   (play 59: the wave-breaker, the muster-point tax); **150** —
   *Fireball* (formations are radius-shaped); **120** — *Fire
   Bolt*, *Dispel Magic*, wand; **100** — *Lightning Bolt*'s
   line (columns and ladders); **60** — everything else. Joe
   engages a battle in *phases*, each range unlocking a shelf.
2. Targeting needs Joe's *own* eyes on the point — height is
   ammunition: the wall, the tower, the hill (Cow spots and
   reports, but the casting sight-line is Joe's; plays 90's
   protocol between volleys).
3. Economy of scale honesty: eleven slots don't defeat an army —
   they defeat an army's **schedule**: the ladder party at the
   wall, the ram crew, the officer cluster (Study the banners,
   +10 — command *is* a cluster), the counter-battery caster.
   Joe's day of slots, spent on choke-moments, is worth a
   company; spent on the mass, it's worth a volley.
4. And the investigation continues in macro: sap-lines, supply
   trains, and camp layouts are documents at 300 feet (play 76
   through a spyglass) — the sortie Joe plans from reading the
   enemy camp beats the ten Fireballs he didn't have.

### At the table

Mechanically: "From this tower I can see their muster square —
that's in Ice Storm range, and it stays difficult terrain
through the assembly they're now doing twice. Fireball's shelf
opens when the ladder parties hit the ditch. I'm not fighting
their army; I'm fighting their timetable, and hail is my
opening memorandum."

Verbally: *"The siege captains plan in hours; the man on the
tower bills them in minutes — a storm on the muster, fire at
the ditch, and every schedule in their camp quietly rewritten
by someone they have not yet seen."*
