Research as a dungeon: hostile stacks, indifferent clerks, and
somewhere in ten thousand pages, the three that matter.

### When to use it

- The case's answer is in records — land titles, ship
  manifests, court rolls, the toy's provenance trail (Joe's own
  campaign-length hunt, plays 78 and 100's counter-file).

### Mechanics

1. **Access is a social dungeon first**: the archivist is the
   door — Prestidigitation's comforts (play 19), genuine
   scholarly credentials (Sage background: Joe *is* the
   colleague, not a tourist), and the forged reader's pass only
   when honesty fails and stakes warrant (play 84's rails).
2. **Search strategy beats search stamina**: Investigation
   (+10) on the *catalog* before the shelves — archives
   organize by their founders' logic (guild? crown? temple?);
   History (+7) reconstructs it, and the right drawer beats a
   week of wrong aisles. Ask the DM to price it honestly:
   method should compress the montage.
3. The paired-language edge: Goblin and Orc sources sit
   *un-catalogued* in human archives ("miscellaneous foreign") —
   Joe's languages open the shelf nobody else could read,
   which is where mistranslated history keeps its originals
   (his backstory says exactly this; make the DM's day by
   asking for that shelf).
4. **Copy, don't borrow**: calligrapher's supplies reproduce
   the page *exactly* (play 83's kit, in its honest mode) —
   the original stays (archives notice), the facsimile
   travels, and the journal notes folio and shelf for the day
   testimony needs a source (play 142).
5. Leaving: *Alarm* is rude in a public archive — instead, a
   hair across the relevant drawer (the mundane tamper-tell)
   and a warm goodbye to the archivist, who will *remember who
   else asked* — the standing wiretap no spell matches.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The manifest's in here somewhere. Method: I
charm the archivist honestly — colleague to colleague — read
their catalog's logic first, and I want the uncatalogued
foreign shelf; if there's an Orcish original of that treaty,
the mistranslation everyone cites falls apart. Everything I
find gets copied, cited, and left in place. And note who the
archivist says visited before us."

Verbally: *"Other adventurers loot tombs. Joe looses himself
into the stacks with a pot of ink and a dangerous amount of
patience — and walks out at closing time holding, in fair
copy, the sentence someone spent forty years hoping stayed
mistranslated."*
