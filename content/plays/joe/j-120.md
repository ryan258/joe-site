Some rooms are worth more than the fight in them. Standing orders
for battle amid the irreplaceable.

### When to use it

- Archives, libraries, laboratories, reliquaries, galleries — and
  every crime scene, where the *room itself* is the witness.

### Mechanics

1. Declared at the door, every time: **no fire, no lightning, no
   Thunderwave** (the shove breaks shelves; the boom shatters
   glass) — the loud column is off. What remains is precise:
   wand darts (they hit only what they're sent at), *Ray of
   Frost*, *Web* (silk preserves; it's the conservator's control
   spell), *Grease* on stone floors only.
2. Crime-scene corollary: AoE *destroys evidence* — a Fireball
   into the study answers this week's fight and burns next
   month's case. Joe fights in evidence rooms like a surgeon
   operates: smallest instrument that resolves.
3. The enemy knows no such rule — which is leverage both ways:
   they may hostage the shelves ("one more step and the archive
   burns"), and *their* recklessness marks what they don't value —
   watch what they're careful around; it's a confession (play 76
   reads the room during the fight).
4. Web's finest hour: a Restrained enemy among the stacks has
   damaged nothing, dropped nothing, and will answer questions
   (play 85) surrounded by the undamaged reasons he came.

### At the table

Mechanically: "We're fighting in four hundred years of paper —
loud column's off, all of it. Wand and frost from me, Web the
moment one of them bunches, and watch which shelf they keep
between themselves and Brenna: they're protecting something, and
afterward I want to know what."

Verbally: *"The thief expects fire — wizards mean fire. Instead
the stacks fill with patient silk, and he is caught, gently, like
a moth in the one room where Joe would never bring a flame."*
