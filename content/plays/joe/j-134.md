Someone's at zero. The next six seconds run on procedure, not
adrenaline.

### When to use it

- Any ally drops. The protocol pre-answers the panic: who moves,
  who covers, what Joe casts.

### Mechanics

1. Triage the *threat*, not the body first: death saves take
   three failures — a body left alone is stable-ish for rounds;
   a body **being attacked** takes auto-crit failures up close.
   Joe's first job is making the body boring: **Web between the
   downed and the enemy** (a Restrained executioner executes
   nothing), frost on the approaching finisher (play 9's
   rearguard, pointed inward).
2. The healing division of labor: the *nearest* ally feeds the
   potion (Bonus Action, play 68) or the healer heals — Joe's 120-foot
   kit almost never makes him the medic; his slot buys the
   medic's safe approach instead (the corridor of Web and
   images the cleric walks through).
3. **Sculpted fire over the body** is the signature version
   (play 1): enemies cluster over downed prey — a Fireball
   centered on the pile, downed ally sculpted out (unconscious
   allies are still creatures Joe can see: still sculptable),
   is the single most Joe answer to a death-pile there is.
4. Afterward, the audit (play 96's discipline, post-hoc): *why*
   did they drop — focus fire? a leak in the formation? — one
   sentence at the next rest, because the protocol's real goal
   is never running it twice for the same reason.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Tam's down — protocol: Web lands between him and
the two coming to finish it, Restrained or at minimum walled.
Senna, you're closest — your Bonus Action is the potion: feed him,
then use your Action for your sword. And the pile forming over him is
exactly Fireball-shaped: Tam's sculpted, they're not."

Verbally: *"The raiders close on the fallen man like a
sentence ending — and meet silk, then frost, then a sphere of
fire that somehow, impossibly, is everywhere except the six
feet of ground where a friend lies breathing."*
