Marching order is a spell Joe casts on the whole day. Ten feet
of spacing is the cheapest AC in the game.

### When to use it

- Every corridor, road, and door — standing doctrine, re-briefed
  when the party gets sloppy (they will).

### Mechanics

1. Joe's slot in the file: **center-rear** — behind the front
   line's reach, inside 60 feet of everything (the kit's
   densest range band), never *last* (rear attacks exist and
   the wizard isn't the rearguard; the ranger is).
2. The spacing paradox, resolved: **anti-Fireball spacing**
   (10–15 feet between allies beats one enemy AoE catching
   four) versus **Sculpt convenience** (Joe *wants* allies
   clustered where his own AoE lands) — the resolution is
   *who clusters*: the front line fights tight **at the
   enemy** (inside Joe's sculpted zones, plays 1, 74), the
   back line strings loose (nobody's AoE should solve the
   support row). Tight where Joe aims, loose where enemies
   would.
3. Doors are the doctrine's exam (play 96 begins there): the
   party stacks *beside*, never *in*, the frame (dragon's
   breath is door-shaped); Joe's control pre-targets the
   *inside* threshold — the room's welcome mat is his, silk
   or oil, before the second ally crosses.
4. The brief travels: hirelings, rescued prisoners, and the
   week's NPC all get the two sentences ("stay ten feet from
   each other; if fire comes, it will not come where you
   stand if you're beside Brenna") — Sculpt's count covers
   the innocent *when Joe can see and name them* (play 74);
   formation is how he always can.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Order for the vault level: Brenna and the
lantern first, then Senna at ten feet, me center with the
prisoner — she stays touching my left sleeve, that's a Sculpt
guarantee — Tam anchors. At doors: stack the hinge side,
nobody in frame, and the welcome mat inside is mine before
second entry. Same as yesterday. It'll be the same tomorrow."

Verbally: *"The party moves down the corridor like a sentence
Joe has diagrammed — every clause in its place, ten feet of
grammar between them — and somewhere ahead, a trap built for
a crowd waits for a crowd that never comes."*
