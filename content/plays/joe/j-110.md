Cover, elevation, and range — the enemy playing Joe's own game, and
how to make them regret the flattery.

### When to use it

- Crossbows behind battlements, archers in windows, the ambush from
  the treeline.

### Mechanics

1. **AoE ignores cover bonuses to AC**: a *Fireball* needs no attack
   roll — the battlement that gives +2 AC against arrows does nothing
   against a save-or-burn filling the whole window. (Total cover
   still blocks targeting; a *point* Joe can see suffices, and
   murder-holes helpfully provide them.)
2. Deny their eyes before crossing ground: *Web* across the firing
   slit's face, Minor Illusion's false crate-line (play 14) for the
   party's advance, Gust of Wind's push making exposed walkways
   untenable (play 45 — swapped in when sieges loom).
3. Joe's counter-battery discipline: he outranges shortbows (*Fire
   Bolt* 120, *Fireball* 150) and fights from Three-Quarters Cover
   himself — the duel is symmetric and Joe's cover doesn't stop
   *their* arrows less than theirs stops his saves.
4. Cow maps the nests first (play 5): the count, the reloading
   rhythm, the ladder behind the wall — one circuit converts "arrows
   from somewhere" into a target list.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow counts five in the gatehouse loft, one window.
Their cover's worth plus-two against arrows and worth nothing
against this: Fireball, through the window, centered inside the
loft. Dex save DC 15 in a room that size — there is no 'behind
cover' in there."

Verbally: *"The archers built their nest against arrows, and against
arrows it is excellent. What comes through the window instead
arrives as a sphere and checks every corner personally."*
