Sometimes Joe's whole action is worth more in someone else's
hands. Knowing when is a play.

### When to use it

- High-AC target, low party hit-rates, and an ally whose hit is
  worth double Joe's cantrip (the rogue's Sneak Attack pile, the
  smite, the maneuver rider).

### Mechanics

1. The trade math: vs AC 19, Joe's Fire Bolt expects about **8.5**
   damage (with Potent Cantrip's floor and critical hits); the
   **Help action** converting the
   rogue's miss into a hit swings **3d6+dex+2d6 Sneak** — when
   the befriended number is bigger than Joe's own, Help *is*
   the damage spell.
2. Joe's Help at range... isn't: 2024 Help-as-distraction needs
   Joe **within 5 feet** of the target — which is exactly where
   Joe never stands. The wizard's *actual* Help economy runs
   through **Cow** (play 91: the flyby is airborne Help with an
   exit) — Joe's own action stays ranged, Cow's covers the
   adjacency.
3. The non-combat Help economy is enormous and free: Joe
   assisting the party-face's Persuasion with exhibits (play
   80), the scout's Stealth with the route brief and snuffed
   lanterns (play 82), the healer's medicine with Investigation
   on the symptoms — Advantage granted by *genuine, described
   assistance* is the cheapest magic on the sheet.
4. The discipline: Help is never the lazy default — it's a
   priced decision that beats a cantrip *specifically* when the
   assisted number is bigger. Do the multiplication; then
   commit.

### At the table

Mechanically: "AC nineteen and my bolts are bouncing — new
allocation: Cow flies the distraction on it, so Senna's
Advantage is live, and her hit is worth four of mine. My
action goes to the wand instead — force darts don't roll.
Between her knife and my arithmetic, it's a better round than
two more misses."

Verbally: *"Joe's proudest rounds are sometimes the quiet ones —
a bird where it's inconvenient, three unavoidable lights, and
the credit gone entirely, by design, to the knife he made
un-missable."*
