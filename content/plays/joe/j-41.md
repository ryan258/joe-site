Three duplicates make Joe the most attractive wrong answer on the
battlefield.

### When to use it

- The party needs enemy attacks pointed somewhere harmless for two or
  three rounds — protecting a hurt ally, a ritual, a fallback.
- Enemies with many weak attacks (bandit volleys, stirge swarms) that
  images eat efficiently.

### Mechanics

1. Action, self, 1 minute, **no Concentration**: three illusory
   duplicates mirror Joe's movements.
2. Each time a creature hits Joe with an attack roll, roll a d6 for each remaining
   duplicate. If any d6 rolls a **3 or higher**, one duplicate is hit instead
   of Joe and is destroyed. Duplicates have **AC 12**.
3. Then Joe stands somewhere *visible* and looks valuable — bait that
   hides is wasted bait. The Free Shield (play 3) backstops the attacks
   that get through the images.
4. Immune to the trick: creatures that can't see, or that perceive
   without sight (Blindsight) — check for eyeless enemies before
   volunteering.
5. No Concentration means Joe baits *while* holding *Web* — the full
   tank turn: Web the corridor, Mirror Image, stand at the web's edge
   looking like the problem.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mirror Image — three duplicates, no Concentration, so Web
stays up. Then I step into the open. If an attack hits my AC: I roll 3d6;
on a 3 or higher on any of them, it hits a duplicate at AC 12 instead."

Verbally: *"Four identical scholars regard the raiders with identical
patience. Somewhere in the arithmetic of arrows and sixes, the real one
is taking notes."*
