Eleven slots, one day. The plays upstream of every play.

### When to use it

- At dawn (with play 70) and at every "do we push on?" debate. A
  wizard who budgets by feel runs dry in the second fight.

### Mechanics

1. The book: **four** 1st, **three** 2nd, **three** 3rd, **one** 4th —
   eleven slots plus wand charges plus cantrips-forever.
2. Standing pre-commitments: one 1st to *Mage Armor* (play 34); one
   3rd held for *Counterspell* while any enemy caster might exist; the
   4th parked for the day's flagship (*Ice Storm* / *Dimension Door* /
   *Arcane Eye* — one of them, chosen by the day's shape).
3. That leaves discretionary: three 1sts (Shield-after-the-free-one,
   Grease), three 2nds (Web, Misty Step, Mirror Image), two 3rds
   (Fireball money).
4. Pace by expected fights: two-fight day → spend freely; four-fight
   day → cantrips and wand carry the easy fights *whole*, slots buy
   only the hard ones. Arcane Recovery (play 57) refunds 4 levels at
   the first Short Rest — plan it into the budget, not as a windfall.
5. The end-of-day audit: slots left over after the last fight were
   *Fireballs the party never got*. Err toward spending in the fight
   that is actually dangerous.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Two fights left today at most. So: this fight gets a
real budget — Web now, Fireball when they bunch — and I keep one 3rd
for Counterspell and the 4th for the extraction, because the plan says
we might need to leave through a wall."

Verbally: *"Joe rations magic the way a ship's master rations water:
generously enough to work, precisely enough to arrive."*
