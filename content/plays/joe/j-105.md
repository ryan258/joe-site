The shapeshifter is a forgery with legs, and Joe authenticates
forgeries for a living.

### When to use it

- Anyone's identity is in doubt: the friend back from being separated,
  the official acting off, the classic "which one is real."

### Mechanics

1. The forgery frame (play 83, applied to people): shapeshifters copy
   *appearance*, not *knowledge*. Authentication questions beat
   observation: things only the real one knows, verified against
   Joe's **written journal**, not memory — memory is what imposters
   bet against.
2. *Message* (play 11) runs the check privately — the suspect answers
   where a listening imposter-partner can't coach, and a wrong answer
   doesn't tip the room that Joe knows.
3. Cow's channel (play 79): a Fey spirit's read of a person's
   *feel* — the DM decides what the link senses, but "does Cow feel
   the same person we camped with?" is exactly the fragmentary-clue
   territory the sheet gives Cow.
4. Insight (+3) is Joe's weakest sensor — run the *procedure* instead:
   logged questions, private channel, Cow, then Investigation (+10)
   on the physical tells (wrong calluses, unworn boots, a scar that
   healed twenty years too well).
5. Pre-commitment beats detection: agree party code-phrases *now*, in
   downtime, and log them in the journal. The play you set up before
   the doppelganger arrives is the one that works.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before anyone relaxes: Message, privately, to 'Tam' —
'What did you owe me after the card game in Harrenford?' The real Tam
grumbles the number. An imposter improvises. And Cow's watching from
my pack — I want the feel of the answer, too."

Verbally: *"Joe greets his returned friend warmly, pours the wine,
and — in a whisper only one of them can hear — asks the question with
teeth. The imposter's whole art is faces. Joe has never once
authenticated a face."*
