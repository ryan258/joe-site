Joe carries two daggers. Knowing exactly when steel beats magic takes
one paragraph and saves one life.

### When to use it

- An *Antimagic*-style zone, a silenced room (no Verbal components), or
  bound hands (no Somatic) — the situations where the spell list is a
  paperweight.
- Truly out: slots gone, wand at its last charge, cantrips jammed.

### Mechanics

1. Dagger: Simple weapon (proficient), Finesse — attack at **+5** (Dex
   +2, prof +3), damage **1d4+2** piercing. Thrown 20/60 with the same
   numbers.
2. Two daggers, but no meaningful two-weapon play (the sheet's Nick
   note applies: the mastery isn't available) — the second dagger is
   the one Joe throws or the one someone takes from him first.
3. *Silence* jams *every* spell Joe owns (all have Verbal components)
   — the dagger and the wand (**no components**, just activation) are
   the entire silenced-Joe toolkit. Remember the wand first.
4. This play is mostly a reminder wrapped in a stat line: the answer
   to "Joe can't cast" is *move, wand, dagger* — in that order.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Silence field — no spells at all. But the wand isn't a
spell I'm casting with components: one charge, three darts. If the
wand's dry, dagger at +5 and I'm backing toward the door."

Verbally: *"Stripped of his library, Joe holds the knife like an
uncomfortable quotation — accurate, brief, and not his preferred
source."*
