Investigation +10 is the highest number on the sheet. The Study action
is how it gets rolled.

### When to use it

- Any *obtained* evidence whose meaning is the question: documents,
  mechanisms, wounds, architecture, residue, ledgers.
- Per the framework (and `Joe's Investigation Framework.md`): Perception
  *notices*; Investigation *interprets*. Roll the right one.

### Mechanics

1. **Study action**: an Intelligence (Investigation) check at **+10**
   to deduce from clues — how the mechanism works, when the letter was
   really written, what made these wounds, which wall is newer.
2. Passive Investigation 20 is a number D&D Beyond displays, not a
   rule. The 2024 glossary defines Passive Perception; there is no
   mandatory Passive Investigation floor. Some DMs adopt one as a
   convention, and that is theirs to offer, not Joe's to invoke.
   Joe *states intent and method* ("I'm comparing the ink on these two
   pages") and lets the DM call for the roll. The statement of method
   is what earns the +10 its purchase.
3. Preparation multiplies it: Prestidigitation's evidence kit (play
   18), good light, time, and the calligrapher's-supplies synergy on
   documents (play 83 — argue Advantage when the tool genuinely
   applies).
4. The chain: Search/Perception finds the thing (party job — Joe's
   Perception is +3), *Study* reads the thing (Joe's job), Persuasion
   spends the reading (play 80).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Now that we've got the ledger: Study action. Method — I'm
cross-checking the shipping dates against the tide tables in its own
appendix, because a forger fakes signatures, not tides. Investigation:
27."

Verbally: *"Joe reads the ledger twice. The first time like its author.
The second time like its author's enemy."*
