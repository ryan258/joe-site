The monster that punishes touching it, fought by the man who never
touches anything.

### When to use it

- Gelatinous, black, grey, or ochre things — anything that corrodes
  weapons, splits when cut, or engulfs.

### Mechanics

1. Standing order the moment Arcana (+7) says "ooze": **nobody
   melees** until the type is named — corrosion eats the fighter's
   sword, and slashing famously *splits* some of them into two
   problems.
2. Joe's kit is ooze-proof by design: *Fire Bolt* from 120 feet,
   the wand's darts, *Fireball* for the big ones — fire neither
   splits nor corrodes-back.
3. Oozes are slow (Speed 10–20 typical): *Ray of Frost* makes them
   effectively stationary hazards, and the party simply walks —
   kiting doctrine (play 8) at its most absurdly effective.
4. Mage Hand (10 lb) pokes what must be poked; the ceiling check is
   ooze SOP (grey hunters drop) — Cow's darkvision sweep overhead
   before the party crosses any ooze-country chamber (play 5).
5. The engulfed-ally emergency: fire damage on the ooze, not the
   ally — *Scorching Ray*'s precision (play 42) beats a *Burning
   Hands* that catches both.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Ceiling first — Cow says clear. That puddle is the
floor-kind, Speed ten: nobody swing at it, it splits. Ray of Frost
holds it at effectively zero movement and we walk around the long
way while my Fire Bolt does the whole fight from here."

Verbally: *"The ooze waits with the patience of chemistry. Joe
declines the experiment, and burns it from a distance that respects
the syllabus."*
