The heist verb: distance-and-direction teleportation makes walls a
suggestion.

### When to use it

- The vault, cell, or shrine is sealed and the seal is the hard part —
  and its interior dimensions are known or reliably mapped.
- Extraction *into* a place, then out via the same slot tomorrow, is
  the whole operation.

### Mechanics

1. "Describe by distance and direction" is the burglar's clause: "15
   feet past this wall, at my elevation" — legal, sight unseen.
2. The 4d6-and-fail clause (play 61) prices bad intelligence: if the
   described spot is occupied (a shelf, a statue, a guard), the wall
   wins. Buy the floor plan first: *Arcane Eye* through the keyhole
   gap (play 63), Cow at the window, Investigation on the architecture
   (+10 — "this tower's floors are 10 feet apart; the vault must be 20
   by 20").
3. One passenger means the specialist comes too — Joe is the door, the
   rogue is the hands.
4. Exit plan before entry: the return trip is a second casting Joe does
   not have today (one 4th slot). In-and-out same day means the way out
   is stairs. Plan it like a Keeper, not a gambler.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Arcane Eye already walked the vault: twenty by twenty,
empty center. Dimension Door — Senna and me, 'thirty feet ahead, through
the wall, ground level.' We're inside. We leave by the coal chute; I
don't have a second casting."

Verbally: *"The vault's builders spent a fortune on the door. Joe files
his objection from the inside."*
