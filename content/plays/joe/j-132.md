Two casters, two Concentrations, one plan for who drops what
when the arrows start landing.

### When to use it

- Any party with a second concentrating caster (cleric's
  *Bless*, druid's terrain, another wizard's anything) — which
  spell survives contact must be decided *before* contact.

### Mechanics

1. The treaty's articles: rank the concurrent spells by
   replacement cost ("my Web is 2nd level and re-castable;
   your *Spirit Guardians* is the fight — you're the protected
   asset"); the *protected* caster gets the safer position, the
   images (play 46's stack has two beneficiaries now), and the
   party's bodyguarding; the *cheaper* concentrator stands
   nearer the risk.
2. Complementary shapes beat redundant ones: Joe's **stop**
   zones (Web/Grease hold enemies *in* a place) multiply the
   ally's **grind** zones (Guardians/Moonbeam damage a place) —
   deliberately overlap them; a held enemy inside a grinding
   aura is the two-caster machine.
3. One-Concentration discipline doubles: Joe holding Web cannot
   *also* Fly, Eye, or Invisibility (the sheet's whole
   Concentration column is mutually exclusive) — the *party's*
   pool is what's now budgeted, out loud, like slots (play 73's
   logic across two sheets).
4. Break-glass clause: if both must drop for a *Counterspell*-
   priority moment — Joe's the designated counterspeller
   (it's not Concentration; the treaty's confusion is common
   and wrong: dropping isn't needed — but the *reaction* is
   his, so his ally's spell never competes with the umbrella).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Treaty terms for this fight: your Guardians
outrank my Web — you hold, I'm the one who re-casts if someone
has to eat a hit. I'm parking my Web exactly under your
aura's edge so they marinate in it, and Counterspell duty is
mine regardless — that's a reaction, not my Concentration."

Verbally: *"The cleric burns like a stationary sun, and Joe —
journeyman of the partnership tonight — quietly rearranges
the enemy into her light, one silk parcel at a time."*
