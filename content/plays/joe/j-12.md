Interview two people at once and compare the stories before they can
synchronize them.

### When to use it

- Two suspects, witnesses, or guards whose accounts should match — if
  they are honest.
- Any interrogation where the framework (play 5) says the lie lives in
  the differences.

### Mechanics

1. The party's talker questions witness A aloud. Joe casts *Message* at
   witness B in another room or across the yard and asks the same
   question.
2. B's whispered reply is private; A never hears the question was asked
   twice. Compare answers in real time.
3. Follow with Insight (+3) on the delivery and Investigation (+10) on
   the contradictions — interpretation is where Joe wins.
4. Cow watching one witness's body language (play 5) makes it a
   three-channel interview.

### At the table

Mechanically: "While Tam questions the steward, I cast Message at the
groom in the stable: 'What time did the coach actually leave?' I want
to compare his answer to the steward's."

Verbally: *"Two rooms, one question, and Joe standing in the hallway
between them like a scale waiting to tip."*
