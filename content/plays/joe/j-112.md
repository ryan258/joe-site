Charges need ground, and ground is the thing Joe edits.

### When to use it

- Mounted enemies, charging beasts, or anything whose plan is a
  straight fast line into the party.

### Mechanics

1. Difficult terrain kills charges at the arithmetic level: **Ice
   Storm** (play 59) drops damage *and* leaves the lane difficult —
   a charge that arrives next round, at half speed, into a set
   line, isn't a charge.
2. *Grease* under the lead horse (play 25): a mount failing a Dex
   save goes Prone at speed — riders get acquainted with the ground
   at the DM's ruling, and the file behind stacks into the pileup.
3. *Web* across the lane is the hard stop where anchors exist; the
   hedgerows and gateposts of farm country are anchor-rich — read
   the ground early.
4. *Thunderwave* is the last-ditch: the 15-foot cube and shove as
   the lance arrives (play 22's button, sized for horses).
5. Timing rule: terrain spells land **in front of** the charge, one
   round early — control cast at the charge's arrival square, not
   at its current one. Lead the target; it's a marksman's habit
   applied to spells.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Riders, forming for a lance charge down the causeway.
They'll cover sixty this round — so the Ice Storm lands on the
*next* sixty, not on them: hail now, and that stretch is difficult
terrain when they hit it. The charge arrives as a walk, and Brenna's
wall of pointy opinions is waiting."

Verbally: *"The charge is magnificent for four seconds. Then the
world under it turns to ice-rubble that wasn't in the plan, and
magnificence becomes a queue."*
