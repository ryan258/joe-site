Small comforts open more doors than Persuasion.

### When to use it

- Building rapport with witnesses, children, innkeepers, and anyone
  cold, tired, or bored.
- Softening a room before the party's talker makes the actual ask.

### Mechanics

1. Warm the guard's soup on a freezing night. Clean the farmer's coat.
   Flavor the terrible rations like his mother's stew. Light the widow's
   fire with a snap.
2. Mechanically these are Prestidigitation's material modes; socially
   they are Advantage-bait — a DM who sees genuine helpfulness often
   grants Advantage on the Persuasion (+3) that follows.
3. Small sensory effects amuse children and mark Joe as harmless — a
   puff of harmless sparks, the smell of fresh bread.
4. This play is Joe's warmth-through-practical-help personality made
   mechanical. Use it before you need anything.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before I ask the gate sergeant about the wagon, I warm
his tea with Prestidigitation and dry his rain-soaked cloak. Then the
question, and I'd argue for Advantage on the Persuasion."

Verbally: *"Steam curls off the sergeant's mug that had gone cold an
hour ago. 'Long watch,' Joe says, and means it."*
