A ship is a wooden box of rigging, lamp-oil, and nowhere to
retreat. The Evoker owns it — or sinks everyone.

### When to use it

- Any fight on, against, or between ships; harbor jobs; river
  crossings under threat.

### Mechanics

1. **Fire discipline is rule zero**: on *your own* ship, Fireball,
   Fire Bolt, and Burning Hands are mutiny-grade decisions —
   tarred rigging and dry canvas ignite (plays 7's power, inverted
   into a hazard). The whole fire column is for *their* ship, and
   only when sinking it is the actual plan.
2. The ship-legal kit: wand darts, *Ray of Frost* (rigging climbers
   descend fast when slowed), *Thunderwave* (boarders → water, the
   sea is the best Prone there is), *Web* between masts and rails —
   a boarding party arriving into rigging-anchored silk is a
   comedy, and *Gust of Wind* astern (ask the DM what it does to
   sails; the answer is usually "yes").
3. Elevation and anchors everywhere: Misty Step to the crow's nest
   (seen, 30 feet — play 39), Alarm on the hold hatch during
   harbor nights (play 31), Cow as the topmast lookout no crew
   matches (Darkvision 120 over black water — play 86).
4. Against *their* ship, the priorities invert into siege work:
   Fire Bolt the sails at 120 feet (objects, flammable, play 7),
   Ice Storm the deck crew from 300 (play 59) — a wizard
   outranges every ballista the DM hasn't invented reasons for.
5. The extraction reality: *Dimension Door* reaches 500 feet —
   shore, or the other ship — and carries one crewmate (play 61).
   Over open water, that's the lifeboat that always launches.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Standing order while we're aboard: nothing of mine
burns — this ship is made of fire fuel and we're standing on it.
Boarders get Thunderwave into the sea and Web in the shrouds. The
*pirate's* ship, though — their mainsail is an object, it's dry,
and Fire Bolt reaches it from here."

Verbally: *"The pirates board through silk and pressure and take
their leave over the rail; and behind them their own canvas
blooms orange, because Joe reserves fire for property he isn't
standing on."*
