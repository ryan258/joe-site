The Evoker in dry country: the most dangerous thing in the woods
is Joe having a normal combat round.

### When to use it

- Forests, grasslands, barns, granaries, tar streets — any map
  where "ignites flammable objects" describes the *terrain*.

### Mechanics

1. The honest audit: *Fireball*, *Fire Bolt*, and *Burning Hands*
   all ignite unattended flammables, and a late-summer forest is
   one continuous unattended flammable. A wildfire doesn't check
   faction — it's tomorrow's problem for allies, villages, and
   the evidence Joe came for.
2. Dry-country loadout (the pivot, play 56): *Ice Storm* and
   *Lightning Bolt* still ignite? — lightning does (play 48);
   hail doesn't. The clean column: **frost, force, thunder,
   control** — Ray of Frost, wand, Thunderwave, Web-and-Grease
   (with the flammability asterisks known and *chosen*, plays 2
   and 25).
3. Fire *as* terrain weapon stays available — deliberately, with
   the wind read and an exit: a backfire line lit by Fire Bolt at
   120 feet is a legal siege of a pursuing force. It's the
   difference between arson and engineering: a plan for where it
   stops.
4. The character owns this rule (it's the Ideal wearing a
   forestry hat): *precise force* — the man who sculpts fire
   around friends doesn't start fires he can't sculpt.

### At the table

Mechanically: "It's been dry for a month and this is pine —
fire's out of my rotation entirely, today's damage is frost and
force. If they push us at the ravine I'll consider one backfire
line, downwind of the village, and only if we can hold the break
while it burns out."

Verbally: *"The bandits assume the wizard's fire is the danger.
The wizard, watching the wind move ten thousand dry trees,
quietly agrees — and reaches for winter instead."*
