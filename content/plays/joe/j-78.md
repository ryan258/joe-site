+7 for the timeline — because *when* is evidence, and Joe's cases run
on timelines.

### When to use it

- Ruins, heraldry, coinage, inscriptions, dynasties, old wars, and the
  provenance chase at the heart of Joe's own hunt (the toy's earliest
  records — his backstory is a History check that never ended).
- Establishing when a site was built, abandoned, re-occupied, or faked.

### Mechanics

1. Intelligence (History) at **+7**: recall historical events, people,
   places, and their material culture — the mason's marks, the coin's
   mint, the treaty the mural celebrates.
2. Combine with the Study action: Investigation (+10) reads the
   *physical* evidence ("this doorway was bricked up later"), History
   (+7) supplies the *context* ("...during the Interdiction, when
   goblin trade was banned — which explains the Goblin curse scratched
   inside"). Two rolls, one conclusion; say which is which.
3. Joe's languages are evidence tools: **Goblin and Orc** open sources
   other scholars mistranslated — flag untranslated text; it is
   probability-one relevant to his own case and often to the party's.
4. Anachronism is the forgery-detector: History says what *should* be
   there; Investigation spots what is. The mismatch is the finding
   (play 83 runs this on documents).

### At the table

Mechanically: "The keystone carries a two-crown mark — History, plus
seven: which dynasty, and was this valley theirs? ...A 19. Then the
mismatch: that mark's two centuries older than these Orcish supply
tallies. Someone re-occupied this fort, recently."

Verbally: *"To the others it's a ruin. To Joe it's a witness with a
long memory and no reason to lie — provided you ask in the right
century, and occasionally in Orc."*
