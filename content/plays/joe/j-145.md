Losing the spellbook is losing the character. The insurance
policy costs 480 gold and gets written before it's needed.

### When to use it

- Downtime, starting now: the project the sheet's own audit
  priced (48 hours and 480 GP for all 48 current spell
  levels; 1 hour and 10 GP per level from one's *own* book).

### Mechanics

1. Priorities under partial funding (the whole book at once
   is rarely affordable): **tier one** — *Mage Armor, Shield,
   Web, Misty Step, Counterspell, Fireball, Dimension Door*
   (the plays-per-page core); **tier two** — the rituals
   (they're the free economy, play 72); **tier three** —
   everything else as gold allows. Twelve levels ≈ 120 GP
   buys tier one: do that *this* downtime.
2. Storage doctrine: the backup lives **nowhere near the
   original** — a bank vault, a trusted archive (play 141's
   archivist earns their tea), sealed and *Alarm*-warded
   where the DM allows standing rituals (play 32). Never in
   the same bag, camp, or city quarter as Joe when avoidable.
3. Operational habits shrink the risk window: the working
   book travels in oilcloth (water, play 116, is the boring
   apocalypse), never leaves Joe's pack in town, and gets
   the tamper-tell (play 141's hair) in any room Joe sleeps
   away from it.
4. The grim arithmetic that justifies it all: without a book,
   Joe re-prepares only what he has — the sheet's whole
   flexibility engine (plays 56, 70) dies with the binding.
   480 GP against the character's entire architecture is not
   a purchase; it's a premium.

### At the table

Mechanically: "This week's downtime is transcription: tier
one goes into the backup — Armor, Shield, Web, Step,
Counterspell, Fireball, Door — twelve spell levels, 120 gold,
twelve evenings. It banks at Harrenford under the archivist's

Verbally: *"Every night for two weeks, the last light in

patiently, like a man writing letters to a future disaster
and telling it: not everything."*
