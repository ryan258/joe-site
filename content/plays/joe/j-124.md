The high city: gap math, tile physics, and the vertical version
of every street play.

### When to use it

- Rooftop approaches, escapes, stakeouts, and the fights that
  find you up there.

### Mechanics

1. Gap math, pre-computed: a running long jump covers Strength-
   score feet (Joe: **8** — he does *not* jump; the sentence is
   the plan); *Misty Step* crosses 30-foot gaps as a Bonus
   Action with a cantrip to spare (plays 39–40) — Joe crosses
   *better* than the athletes, once per slot, and picks routes
   with that budget in mind.
2. Tiles are terrain with opinions: sloped, frost-slick, or
   loose — the DM's roof is often *already* Grease (play 121's
   logic, tilted). Fight from ridge-lines and flat leads;
   shove-plays (118) are lethal up here in both directions.
3. The stakeout stack: Cow holds the higher roost (plays 86,
   88), Joe reads windows with Investigation from across the
   street, Message runs the net silently (play 11), Alarm wards
   the roof-hatch behind the watchers (play 32).
4. Falling is the whole ledger (1d6/10 ft): rooftop *Web* has a
   novel job — cast across the alley *below* a likely fall line
   it's a net (DM adjudicates, but silk built to hold creatures
   holds creatures); Joe rigs it before the parley on the roof,
   because the parley is on a *roof*.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Route brief: we cross at the tannery where the
gap's eight feet — that's a plank, we carry a plank — and the
one twenty-footer is my Misty Step, so I go last and lock the
shutter behind us. Cow's already on the bell-tower. And before
anyone stands on a parley ledge: Web goes across the alley
under it. Call it insurance."

Verbally: *"The tiles whisper every step's cost and Joe pays in
exact change — planks, patience, and one borrowed doorway of
mist — arriving at the eaves above the meeting with the
unhurried air of a man who has already priced every way down."*
