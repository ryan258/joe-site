The full interrogation stack, assembled: every social sensor Joe owns,
run in sequence on one subject.

### When to use it

- The high-stakes conversation: the suspect who'll only talk once, the
  witness leaving town at dawn, the broker who knows about Cow.

### Mechanics

1. **Prepare** (before they sit down): Investigation (+10) on what's
   already known — the questions that *have* checkable answers are the
   trap's teeth. Warm the room, literally (play 19).
2. **Position the sensors**: Cow present-as-toy on the shelf (plays 79,
   88), the party's Wisdom-reader seated opposite, Joe angled to watch
   hands, not eyes.
3. **Run the channels in order**: open questions first (Insight +3 on
   delivery); then the checkable facts (contradictions logged, not
   challenged — yet); *Message* to a second interviewer running the
   same facts elsewhere (play 12); Cow's feeling-tone on each name and
   date the subject touches.
4. **Close with the ledger**: challenge the contradictions all at
   once, in evidence order (play 80's register). People renegotiate
   with the scholar who *counted* — and the DM has watched a
   procedure, not a die roll, which is worth more than either.
5. Never: threats, bait-with-people, or Cow's impressions asserted as
   fact. The sheet's principles are the protocol's walls.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Protocol: Tam pours the tea, I open with the three
questions I can already verify, Insight on the delivery — 12, so I
trust the log more than my read — Message relaying question four to
Senna with the sister in the kitchen, and Cow's on the mantel. After
her fourth answer I lay out the two contradictions, in order, and
wait."

Verbally: *"It never feels like an interrogation. It feels like being
read to — from a book you didn't know you'd written, by a man who has
clearly reached the ending."*
