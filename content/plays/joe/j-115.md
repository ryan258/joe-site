Joe is a Human with no Darkvision. Unmanaged, that's the party's
biggest hidden weakness; managed, it's a rounding error.

### When to use it

- Every dungeon, every night fight, every "the torches go out."
  This is a standing-orders play.

### Mechanics

1. The honest ledger: in darkness Joe is **Blinded** — attack rolls
   at Disadvantage, enemies attack him with Advantage, no *seen*
   points for AoE targeting or *Misty Step*, no wand (visible
   target), no *Counterspell* (see). Nearly the whole kit
   gates on sight. Light is not comfort; light is ammunition.
2. Sources, in preference order: the party's Light-cantrip caster
   (beg one, seriously); lantern and oil (starting gear — hooded,
   for shutter discipline); *Prestidigitation* lights candles and
   small flames free (play 18); **Fire Bolt lights distant things**
   — a brazier, a tar barrel, an enemy's own wagon — at 120 feet
   (play 7): Joe can *install* light in the enemy's half of the
   field.
3. The asymmetry play: light on **them**, dark on **us** — a lit
   enemy is visible to the whole party while the party shoots from
   shadow; Joe's ignition-at-range is the only no-slot way to do
   this from safety.
4. Cow's **Darkvision 120** is the party's night sensor regardless
   (plays 86, 90) — Joe fights blind but never *scouts* blind.
5. Standing order: darkness falling unexpectedly = someone's
   *choice* — assume a reason, Web the likeliest approach, and
   re-light on Joe's schedule, not theirs.

### At the table

Mechanically: "They doused the hall — that's for their benefit, so
we decline: Fire Bolt at the oil sconce on the far wall — it's an
object, it ignites. Their end of the hall is now lit and ours
isn't. Cow's eyes tell me nothing crossed the middle yet. Hold the
line at the shadow's edge."

Verbally: *"Darkness falls, and with it the enemy's assumption
that darkness is theirs. Joe sets their own wall alight from a
hundred feet and stands in the black with his friends, invisible
to the people he has just made famous."*
