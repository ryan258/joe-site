The next milestone is a feat-or-ASI, and the sheet's numbers
already argue the case: Concentration is the hole.

### When to use it

- At the level-up (milestone advancement, DM's schedule) —
  but the *decision* gets made now, in downtime, on paper.

### Mechanics

1. The diagnosis (the file's own audit found it): CON save
   **+3 with no proficiency** guards every Concentration
   spell Joe runs — Web, Fly, Invisibility, Gust, Arcane
   Eye — and plays 46, 54, and 93 all list the same failure
   mode. The candidates:
2. **Resilient (Constitution)**: +1 CON (16→17, HP
   unchanged... odd score banked for 20 later) and **save
   proficiency** — +3 becomes **+6** (**+7 at level 9**), scaling
   with proficiency forever. The pure fix.
3. **War Caster**: **Advantage on Concentration saves**
   (statistically comparable to Resilient at current DCs),
   somatic-components-with-full-hands, and the reaction
   *opportunity-cantrip* — a leaving enemy eats Fire Bolt,
   which rewrites play 58's reaction budget (a third real
   claimant). Broader; doesn't scale the save itself.
4. **+2 Intelligence (18→20)**: DC 15→**16**, attack +7→+8,
   Investigation +10→**+11**, and every save-spell in the
   book tightens — the *offense* reading, arguing the best
   Concentration defense is enemies who failed their saves
   and died.
5. The recommendation on this sheet's evidence: **the
   Concentration fix first** (Resilient if the DM's damage
   runs single-big-hits where +7 flat beats Advantage; War
   Caster if many small hits and reaction value tempt) — INT
   20 lands at level 12 regardless, per play 150's runway.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Milestone's coming, and the file's already
argued it: everything I hold — Web, Fly, the Eye — hangs on
a plus-three save, and plays forty-six, fifty-four, and
ninety-three all named it. Resilient Constitution takes it
to plus-six (plus-seven next level) and scales. Intelligence twenty
can have level twelve. The wizard who never drops the Web doesn't need a
sixteen DC yet."

Verbally: *"Joe levels up the way he does everything —
against the ledger. And the ledger says: the man who holds
the silk steady under fire is worth more, this year, than
the man whose fire burns one degree hotter."*
