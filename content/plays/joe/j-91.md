The one combat job worth Cow's feathers: Advantage, delivered by air
mail, with a clean getaway.

### When to use it

- The party's martial needs Advantage on a decisive attack — the
  rogue's setup, the fighter's swing at the caster — and the sky is
  cheap (no archers, no readied attacks, no hawks).
- Never when incidental AoE is live (play 89 parks Cow instead), and
  never against enemies that punish approach.

### Mechanics

1. Cow **cannot attack** (familiar rule), but can take **other
   actions** — including **Help**: distract a creature within 5 feet,
   granting an ally **Advantage** on the next attack roll against it
   before the start of Cow's next turn.
2. **Flyby** is the engine: Cow provokes **no Opportunity Attacks**
   when flying out of an enemy's reach — swoop to the target, Help,
   and exit the full 60-foot fly speed away. Executed correctly, Cow
   is adjacent to danger for zero of the enemy's attack windows —
   only readied actions and reach threats can answer.
3. Cow rolls its **own initiative** and acts on its own turn (obeying
   Joe's commands), so the timing is real but not guaranteed: the
   Advantage window runs from Cow's Help until the start of Cow's
   next turn — it feeds whichever ally attacks the target inside that
   window. Call it out when Cow's turn comes up ("Advantage on the
   priest until Cow's next turn — whoever's up, use it").
4. The 1 HP ledger, always: one stray *Burning Hands*, one lucky
   readied shot, and it's play 33's hour and incense. Cow flies only
   when Joe would bet a 10 GP portion *and* Cow's dignity on the
   route — and per the sheet, the appearance of a small toy cow
   swooping a warlord is its own reward.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Cow's turn: flyby on the priest — Help action, and
Flyby means the exit provokes nothing; Cow ends sixty feet up. That's
Advantage on her until Cow's next turn — Senna, you act before then,
so it's yours. Me, on my turn: Fire Bolt, same target."

Verbally: *"Something small and impossible — is that a *cow*? —
flutters through the priest's eyeline at the worst moment of her
morning, and the arrow that follows it does not miss."*
