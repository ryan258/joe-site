Scribing scrolls: turning quiet weeks and spare gold into
slots that keep.

### When to use it

- Real downtime (days, not evenings) with access to fine
  inks — converting the calligrapher's craft into portable
  castings.

### Mechanics

1. The 2024 crafting lane: scribing a spell scroll wants
   **proficiency with Calligrapher's Supplies** (Joe's, by
   background) **or Arcana** (Joe's, +7 — he qualifies twice),
   and the spell **prepared** during the work — a 1st-level
   scroll takes 1 day and costs 25 GP,
   scaling steeply by level (table's rates govern; ask once).
2. What to bench, by shape: **emergency verbs** — *Misty
   Step* and *Shield* scrolls are extra panic buttons beyond
   the day's slots; **rituals for speed** — a *Detect Magic*
   scroll casts in one Action when the ritual's 10 minutes
   don't exist (play 72's one gap, patched); **the escort
   special** — *Mage Armor* scrolls armor the week's
   prisoner without Joe's slot (play 137).
3. Scrolls are also the *party's* spell access: another
   caster (class list permitting) can fire Joe's scroll —
   the cleric holding a *Misty Step* scroll is a backline
   escape Joe doesn't have to be alive to authorize.
   Distribute the bench; don't hoard it.
4. And the forger's note: a spell scroll is a *document* —
   Joe's authentication eye (play 83) prices found scrolls
   before anyone casts a stranger's handwriting (a
   *mis-scribed* scroll is the DM's favorite trap; Arcana
   checks the workmanship first).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Two quiet weeks in the tower, so the bench
runs: three Misty Steps and a brace of Shields for the
emergency drawer, one Detect Magic for when ten minutes
doesn't exist, and a Mage Armor that lives in Tam's pack —
he can't cast it, the day's guest usually can't either, but
I can, from his pack, in one Action. Ink well spent."

Verbally: *"The workshop smells of fine ink and hot wax for
a fortnight — and at the end of it, Joe's emergencies come
pre-written, rolled, sealed, and distributed among his
friends like letters of credit drawn on a very specific
bank."*
