**Spellbook only.** The boom is a feature: a signal audible across a
battlefield, a forest, or a district.

### When to use it

- Pre-arranged signals with a split party: "when you hear the thunder,
  go."
- Deliberately pulling a garrison toward Joe and away from the party's
  real approach.

### Mechanics

1. *Thunderwave*'s boom is audible out to **300 feet** — through walls,
   weather, and battle noise, it is the loudest thing Joe can produce on
   demand.
2. Agree on the meaning beforehand; a signal that needs interpretation
   is not a signal.
3. As a lure it costs a 1st-level slot and marks Joe's position
   precisely — pair it with a prepared exit (*Misty Step*, play 38) or
   an illusion at the blast site (play 13) so what responders find is
   not Joe.
4. Cheap alternative when only allies nearby need the cue: *Message*
   (play 11) is silent and free. Thunderwave is for distance and drama.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The deal was thunder means now. I cast Thunderwave at the
empty courtyard wall — 2d8 to nobody, and everyone within 300 feet hears
it. Then Misty Step onto the roofline."

Verbally: *"The thunder rolls across the rooftops, and in three
different alleys, three people start running on schedule."*
