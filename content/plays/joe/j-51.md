Protect another caster's crucial spell when the timing and slot budget allow.
Joe cannot use the old two-slot sequence to defend his own slotted *Fireball*.

### When to use it

- An ally casts a crucial spell and an enemy within 60 feet counters it.
- Joe can see the enemy casting with components, has his Reaction, and has
  not already expended a spell slot to cast a spell on this same turn.

### Mechanics

1. The revised rule allows only one spell slot expended to cast a spell per
   turn. Joe's slotted *Fireball* followed by his own slotted *Counterspell*
   on that turn is illegal, even with his Reaction available.
2. On an ally's turn, Joe can counter the enemy's *Counterspell* if the
   trigger and his resources allow it. The enemy makes a Constitution save
   against DC 15. On failure, its counter fails; the ally's spell can resolve
   subject to any remaining interruptions and its own rules.
3. This uses Joe's Reaction and a level 3+ slot, leaving no *Shield* until
   his Reaction refreshes. This restriction is per turn, not per round.
4. To protect Joe's own slotted spell, prefer casting from beyond the enemy's
   60-foot Counterspell range when possible, or arrange another ally's cover.

### At the table

Mechanically: "On our caster's turn, I see the enemy countering within 60 feet.
My Reaction is available and I haven't cast with a slot this turn. I cast
Counterspell: Constitution save against DC 15."

Verbally: *"Finish your sentence. I'll handle the interruption."*
