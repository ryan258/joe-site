The tool proficiency that turns Joe into the party's forensic document
examiner.

### When to use it

- Any load-bearing document: wills, deeds, orders, letters, maps,
  confessions. Assume forgery until the ink says otherwise.
- Paired with the Study action (play 76) — the supplies are *how*, the
  +10 is *the read*.

### Mechanics

1. Joe is proficient with **Calligrapher's Supplies** (and carries
   them). On document questions, that proficiency legitimately applies:
   2024 tool rules let the DM grant a tool-plus-skill synergy —
   argue **Advantage on the Investigation check** when examining
   handwriting, ink, nibwork, and seals with the kit in hand.
2. The method (state it — it's what earns the ruling): compare letter-
   forms against a known sample; test ink age and layering; check the
   seal for re-heating; check the paper's cut against the ledger it
   claims to come from.
3. History (+7) dates the *conventions* — honorifics, dating systems,
   watermarks (play 78); Investigation (+10, likely with Advantage)
   catches the *execution* — hesitation strokes, wrong nib, patched
   descenders.
4. The finding is courtroom-grade because the method was: document the
   comparison in Joe's own journal, in ink, with the kit that proved
   it.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I unroll my calligrapher's kit next to the will and the
baron's known letters. Method: letterform comparison on the signature,
ink-layer check where the bequest was amended. Tool proficiency
applies, so I'd ask for Advantage on the Investigation... 26. The
amendment is a different ink, written over an erasure."

Verbally: *"Joe reads the will the way an undertaker reads a body —
unhurried, respectful, and completely certain it has something to
tell him."*
