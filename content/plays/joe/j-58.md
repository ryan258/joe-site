Joe has one reaction and three claims on it. Deciding *before* the
round is the play.

### When to use it

- Every round of every fight. The reaction is a slot that refills each
  turn; an unplanned reaction is usually a wasted one.

### Mechanics

1. The claimants: **Shield** (play 3, vs attacks/Magic Missile),
   **Counterspell** (play 4, vs casting within 60 feet), and
   Opportunity Attacks (never — Joe's dagger reaction is a rounding
   error; plan as if he has two claimants).
2. Default policy by enemy composition: caster present → the reaction
   is *Counterspell* until the caster is down, and Joe's AC relies on
   *Mage Armor*/*Mirror Image*/position. No caster → the reaction is
   *Shield*, threshold "hit by 5 or less."
3. Announce the policy to yourself at initiative: "this fight, my
   reaction is X." Re-evaluate when the caster dies or the bruiser
   closes.
4. The tie case — caster *and* melee pressure — resolves by what ends
   the fight sooner: denying their *Hypnotic Pattern* beats negating
   one greataxe hit, almost always.
5. Track it out loud after spending: "my reaction's gone until my
   turn" — the table (and the DM's monsters) will test the window.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Their priest is still up, so standing policy: my
reaction is reserved for Counterspell, and I'm eating attack rolls on
AC 15 plus images. The moment she drops, I switch back to Shield
duty."

Verbally: *"Joe fights the way he files: one hand always free for the
one document that cannot be allowed through."*
