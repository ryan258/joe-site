Assassins get one cheap round. This is the standing architecture
that makes it expensive.

### When to use it

- Always — it's a *stack*, assembled from habits already running:
  the plays below are cross-references, this is the assembly.

### Mechanics

1. **Detection layer**: *Alarm* on every rest and every occupied
   room (plays 31–32); Cow's watch and Fey senses (play 86); the
   party's Perception disposed by watch schedule — Joe's +3 never
   stands watch alone.
2. **Pre-damage layer**: *Mage Armor* always on during danger-hours
   (play 34) — AC 15 is the difference between an assassin needing
   a 10 and needing a 15; Free *Shield* loaded (play 3) turns the
   surprise hit into a miss once per day, *even in the surprise
   round* — reactions work when Joe isn't Incapacitated, and being
   surprised in 2024 costs Initiative Disadvantage, not your
   reaction.
3. **Post-contact layer**: *Misty Step* pre-visualized (play 38's
   habit — always know the seen exit square); *Mirror Image* as the
   first action of any fight that starts with Joe as the target;
   Thunderwave for the pile-on (play 22).
4. The investigation prevents the sequel: ambushers are *evidence*
   (who knew the route? who paid?) — play 85 on the survivor, and
   the route-leak problem gets solved as a case, not a coincidence.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Crossbow from the loft — surprise round, but: Mage
Armor's been on since the gates, so that's against 15... hits
anyway — reaction, free Shield, now it's against 20. A miss. My
turn now exists: Mirror Image, and somebody take that loft. We're
keeping the crossbowman breathing; he knows who bought the route."

Verbally: *"The bolt was aimed at a scholar's soft evening. It
arrives, instead, at a bureaucracy of prepared refusals — and
somewhere behind the third one, Joe is already composing the
questions for its owner."*
