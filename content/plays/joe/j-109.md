Every heal undoes a party turn. The healer is therefore the target,
whatever the damage says.

### When to use it

- The enemy line keeps standing back up, or a robed figure keeps
  waving at the wounded.

### Mechanics

1. Target priority is a *rate* argument: a healer restoring 10 HP a
   round erases one martial's turn per round — killing the bruiser
   first means fighting the bruiser twice.
2. The toolkit, in preference order: *Counterspell* the heal itself
   (play 4 — a healing word denied is tempo stolen at reaction
   price); *Web* the healer (a Restrained healer needs line of sight
   and freedom it no longer has); focused *Scorching Ray*/wand to
   simply end the position (plays 42, 66 if they're concentrating).
3. Geometry tell: healers stand where they can see the most allies —
   which is a *Fireball*-shaped fact. The cluster around the healer
   is usually the fight's best 20-foot radius.
4. Say the priority out loud at round one (play 96's script): the
   whole party pivoting one round earlier is worth more than any
   single spell Joe casts at it.

### At the table

Mechanically: "That's twice their captain's gotten up. Priority
change, everyone: the grey robe in back. My reaction is Counterspell
on her next heal, Web lands on her square this turn, and notice
she's standing in the middle of all four of them — which is also
where my Fireball's going."

Verbally: *"Joe stops fighting the soldiers and starts fighting the
arithmetic — and the arithmetic wears grey robes and has been having
a very comfortable battle until exactly now."*
