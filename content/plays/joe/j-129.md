*Web* and *Grease* are Sneak Attack dispensers. Run the supply
chain deliberately.

### When to use it

- The party has a rogue (or any Advantage-hungry attacker) and
  Joe's control is landing anyway — the combo is free.

### Mechanics

1. The engine: **Restrained** (Web) grants attackers **Advantage**
   — and Advantage is one of Sneak Attack's two switches. Every
   enemy Joe webs is a fully-armed Sneak Attack ticket, no ally
   adjacency required, at range, every round the silk holds.
2. **Prone** (Grease) grants Advantage only to attacks from
   **within 5 feet** — the melee rogue's version; ranged attacks
   against Prone suffer Disadvantage instead. Match the control
   to the rogue's style: silk for the sniper, oil for the
   knife-worker.
3. Sequencing at initiative (play 96's script, with a
   beneficiary): Joe's control wants to land *before* the rogue's
   turn in the round — compare initiative counts out loud at
   round one and re-order targets accordingly. The same Web is
   worth double landing one count earlier.
4. Announce the product: "Restrained — that's Advantage, which is
   Sneak Attack" — new tables genuinely miss it, and the rogue's
   d6 pile is the best damage-per-slot conversion Joe's 2nd-level
   money can buy.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Senna, I'm before you in the order, so standing
deal: whatever's wrapped in silk when your turn comes is
pre-paid Advantage — full Sneak Attack, from the balcony, no
setup. This round it's the bodyguard. Enjoy."

Verbally: *"Joe's silk doesn't kill anyone. It just holds them
gently, precisely still — like a jeweler steadying the stone —
for the knife that does the engraving."*
