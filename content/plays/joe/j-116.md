Water re-prices the entire kit. Know the new menu before the ship
tilts.

### When to use it

- Fights in rivers, flooded chambers, harbors, or driving storms —
  anywhere "fully immersed" or "soaked" describes a combatant.

### Mechanics

1. The classic ruling (2014 DMG underwater rules; confirm your
   table keeps them): fully **immersed creatures resist fire** —
   Fire Bolt and Fireball halve before resistances even start.
   Underwater is cold-and-force country: *Ray of Frost*, the wand,
   *Ice Storm* from above the surface.
2. **Thunderwave under water** is the sleeper: thunder is
   pressure, water carries it gloriously (flavor), and the
   push-plus-2d8 needs no attack roll a swimming wizard would
   fumble.
3. Somatic and verbal components work underwater at most tables,
   but *breathing* doesn't — Joe's plans stop at the surface unless
   someone else provides the water-breathing; know the party's
   answer before the harbor job, not during.
4. Rain and soaking (the milder case): the DM may rule soaked
   targets or gear resist or extinguish small fires — the oil plays
   (10, 69) die in the rain; frost and force don't. Weather is a
   loadout input (play 56's pivot logic).
5. *Web* underwater is silly (ask anyway; some DMs enjoy it);
   *Grease* underwater is sillier. Control shifts to *Gust of
   Wind* at the surface — waves, per DM — and to terrain.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Half the fight's in the canal, so menu change:
fire's resisted when they're under, so it's Ray of Frost and wand
darts at the swimmers, and if three of them climb our barge at
once — Thunderwave, and the canal can have them back."

Verbally: *"The water takes the heat out of Joe's favorite
arguments, so he switches dialects — frost, force, and pressure —
and the canal discovers he was always multilingual."*
