Streets are a control map at running speed — and nobody controls
a map like the man with silk, oil, and a bird.

### When to use it

- Chasing a suspect through town, or being chased; rooftop
  parkour economies; the fleeing-witness problem.

### Mechanics

1. **Chasing**: never outrun what you can out-think — *Ray of
   Frost* (-10) beats sprinting (the gap closes 10 a round with
   zero Athletics); *Web* across the alley mouth *ahead* of the
   runner (lead the target — play 112's marksman habit, at
   street scale); *Grease* under them at the corner where they
   must slow; and **Cow flies the parallel** (60 speed, above
   the crowd) so losing sight never means losing the target —
   the runner escapes the man and not the sky.
2. **Being chased**: the same board reversed — Grease behind at
   the stair-corner (play 26), Web the arch, Minor Illusion's
   crate-wall where the pursuit must choose doors (play 14),
   Misty Step across the canal that costs them the long way
   round (play 39).
3. Crowds are terrain that thinks: Thunderwave and Fireball are
   *civilian* words in a market — the chase kit is the precise
   column (frost, silk, illusion), for the same reason as the
   Library Rule (play 120): the street is full of people Joe's
   Ideal covers.
4. The arrest is an interview with better staging (play 85):
   a runner Webbed in a cul-de-sac, winded and upright, is a
   *conversation*; a runner tackled by the fighter is a
   hospital visit and a lawyer.

### At the table

Mechanically: "He's rabbiting — Cow's up, tracking him over the
stalls, so run smart, not fast. He'll hit the chandler's alley
in two corners: Web goes across its far mouth *now*, on Cow's
sightline call... he's in it. Restrained, unhurt, and about to
have the least fun honest talk of his week."

Verbally: *"The cutpurse knows every alley in the quarter. He
does not know the sky has been reading over his shoulder since
the fountain, or that thirty feet of the future is already
silk."*
