Cold country: where tracks confess, fires beacon, and white
ground makes every rule legible.

### When to use it

- Winter travel, mountain passes, tundra jobs — persistent snow
  as the operating surface.

### Mechanics

1. **Snow is evidence that falls from the sky**: every approach
   to camp writes itself (Study the tracks: count, gait, weight,
   hours — +10 turns a trail into a manifest); Joe's own party's
   trail is equally legible — route discipline, and Gust of Wind
   (swapped in) can blur a backtrail's story at DM's discretion.
2. Deep snow is difficult terrain: bog arithmetic applies whole
   (play 121) — frost-kiting in snowshoe country is nearly
   unfair.
3. Fire discipline inverts from the forest (play 119): little
   here burns — the fire column is *unlocked* — but flame at
   night in white country is a beacon for miles: heat when
   tactical, dark camps when hunted, Prestidigitation *warming*
   (play 19's mode) as the no-light heat source that keeps
   fingers casting-capable.
4. Ice underfoot is free Grease: fights near frozen water hand
   Joe Prone-geometry without the slot — Thunderwave's shove
   onto lake ice (thickness per DM) is play 118 with a trapdoor.
5. Whiteout is play 115's darkness in daylight — same doctrine:
   Cow above the snow-glare, tight formation, light... sound
   discipline instead (Message net, play 11).

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we break camp: Study on those tracks
crossing ours — four sets, laden, dragging something, under six
hours. And tonight, no fire: Prestidigitation keeps the stew
and our hands warm without announcing us to whoever's dragging
sledges through our valley."

Verbally: *"Winter is Joe's kind of witness — it remembers
everyone who passes and shows its notes to anyone patient
enough to kneel and read. He kneels. He reads. He is very
patient."*
