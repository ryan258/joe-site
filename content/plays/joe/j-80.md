Joe's Persuasion is +3. His Investigation is +10. The play is making
the second number do the first number's job.

### When to use it

- Convincing officials, patrons, and skeptics of a conclusion Joe can
  *prove* — the argument-from-evidence is his native register.
- Any social check where laying out documented findings could earn
  Advantage or replace the roll entirely.

### Mechanics

1. Sequence: investigate first (plays 76–78), *then* persuade — walking
   in with the forged page, the mismatched dates, and the named ritual
   converts a Persuasion gamble into a presentation.
2. Mechanically, argue the frame: strong evidence justifies Advantage
   on the +3 roll, a lowered DC, or — cleanest — the DM ruling that
   facts this good need no roll. Ask for the ruling by *presenting*,
   not by citing bonuses.
3. Division of labor: the party's Charisma character delivers the
   *ask*; Joe delivers the *exhibits* (and Prestidigitation warms the
   room first — play 19). Joe supporting their roll via the Help
   action is often the best use of his turn in a scene.
4. The personality is the technique: quiet, precise, observed-first —
   Joe persuades like a man reading findings into a record, and tables
   reward the register matching the sheet.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I don't argue. I lay out the three exhibits: the tide
table, the forged seal under Prestidigitation's cleaning, the Goblin
original of the mistranslated deed. Then the one sentence: 'The
harbormaster lied.' If that still needs a roll, Persuasion — but I'd
submit the evidence speaks at plus ten, not plus three."

Verbally: *"Joe doesn't raise his voice. He raises documents, one at a
time, in the order that leaves only one available conclusion."*
